# Relationship Source Review

This document records how the supplied relationship astrology resources should inform TLDR Astro knowledge and product logic. It is a source-use note, not app copy.

## Reviewed Resources

- Hayden's Book of Synastry, Ajani Abdul-Khaliq
- Skymates 2: The Composite Chart, Steven Forrest and Jodie Forrest
- Saturn: A New Look at an Old Devil, Liz Greene and Robert Hand
- Fate And Fortune, Ancient Astrology in Theory and Practice

Use these resources for interpretive method and coverage priorities. Do not copy their prose into production content.

## Synastry Method Notes

Hayden is most useful for synastry mechanics.

Core takeaways:

- Synastry is directional. One person's planet activates the other person's planet, angle, or house.
- House overlays describe the area of life being activated, but they are background context.
- Planet-to-planet contacts describe the stronger behavioral exchange.
- Conjunctions are especially important because the two functions tend to play out together automatically.
- A relationship reading should identify the strongest key pair or key pairs instead of treating every aspect equally.
- Both perspectives matter. Person A's planet to Person B's chart is not the same as Person B's planet to Person A's chart.

Product implications:

- Preserve directionality in copy: "{name}'s Moon lands in your 7th" is different from "your Moon lands in {name}'s 7th."
- Rank synastry by more than orb. Weight orb, conjunctions, Sun/Moon/Ascendant, Venus/Mars, Saturn, nodes, angles, and personal planets.
- Use house overlays to answer "where does this person land in my life?"
- Use interaspects to answer "what happens between us?"
- Use the strongest contact as a navigational anchor, not as a verdict.

## Composite Method Notes

Skymates 2 is most useful for composite chart meaning.

Composites is most useful as a practical coverage map for composite planets, houses, aspects, and timing.

Core takeaways:

- Composite describes the relationship as a third entity.
- Composite should not be used to decide which person is doing what.
- Composite is strongest when the relationship has continuity, commitment, or a shared purpose.
- Composite Sun describes the vitality, purpose, and central identity of the bond.
- Composite Moon describes the emotional climate, private world, home feeling, and repair style.
- Composite Ascendant describes how the relationship appears socially and how it functions in public.
- Composite Venus describes affection, kindness, pleasure, courtesy, fondness, and repair.
- Composite Mars describes desire, heat, conflict, anger, and how the relationship handles pressure.
- Composite Saturn describes limits, tests, responsibility, durability, fear, and commitment.
- Composite Mercury describes the relationship's communication pattern, agreements, decision-making, and the way the bond processes information.
- Composite Jupiter describes growth, generosity, faith, shared opportunity, and where the relationship can overpromise.
- Composite Uranus describes disruption, difference, freedom, instability, and the way the relationship resists being overly controlled.
- Composite Neptune describes idealization, longing, imagination, spiritual or artistic sensitivity, confusion, and blurred boundaries.
- Composite Pluto describes intensity, power, control, transformation, compulsion, and what the relationship forces into the open.
- Composite Nodes can describe familiarity, direction, and relationship arc, but language should remain belief-neutral.
- Composite houses show where the relationship expresses itself most visibly. They are especially important for practical interpretation.
- Current transits and progressions to the composite chart can describe relationship timing, but should be framed as pressure on the bond rather than blame on either person.

Product implications:

- Present synastry first for person-to-person dynamics.
- Present composite second for the shared relationship container.
- Do not merge synastry and composite into one generic compatibility score.
- Composite cards should sound like "the relationship does this," not "you are" or "{name} is."
- Composite details should name the bond's need, gift, pressure point, and practical maintenance.
- Composite timing should answer "what is this relationship being asked to deal with now?"

## Saturn Relationship Notes

Saturn is useful for both synastry and composite work, but it needs careful handling.

Core takeaways:

- Saturn can create commitment, continuity, responsibility, loyalty, maturity, and the willingness to keep showing up.
- Saturn can also create fear, inhibition, coldness, guilt, delay, unequal burden, or the feeling that affection has to be earned.
- Saturn contacts often show where the relationship becomes real. That can be stabilizing or heavy depending on how the people involved handle pressure.
- In synastry, the Saturn person may provide structure, seriousness, or containment, but may also become critical, withholding, or controlling.
- In composite charts, Saturn describes the relationship's tests, duties, limits, and long-term maintenance needs.

Product implications:

- Do not describe Saturn as automatic proof of durability.
- Do not describe Saturn as automatically bad.
- Saturn copy should name both the support and the cost.
- Useful reader action: clarify expectations, reduce silent obligation, make the agreement explicit, and notice where warmth has become conditional.

## Traditional Compatibility Notes

Fate And Fortune is most useful for the ranking layer, not reader-facing voice.

Core takeaways:

- Whole-sign houses are the primary house framework for Hellenistic timing and chart relevance.
- Sect, dignity, angularity, and planetary condition modify how easy or difficult a planet's promise is to use.
- Compatibility is not only modern chemistry. Traditional sources weight Moon contacts, Ascendant contacts, benefics, malefics, and house/angle placement.
- Moon-to-Moon or Moon-to-personal-planet harmony is high signal because it affects daily comfort and felt safety.
- Venus contacts can support affection, pleasure, and social ease.
- Jupiter contacts can support generosity, faith, and protection, but can also exaggerate.
- Mars and Saturn contacts can be high signal for attraction, challenge, commitment, pressure, or strain depending on condition and context.
- Contacts to the Ascendant, Midheaven, Lot of Fortune, and angular houses can make a connection more visible or consequential.

Product implications:

- Use traditional rules as ranking modifiers, not fixed compatibility verdicts.
- Boost Moon, Venus, Jupiter, Ascendant, Midheaven, and angular house contacts when close and mutually supportive.
- Treat Mars and Saturn contacts as important, not automatically negative.
- Use sect and dignity to adjust tone before writing copy.
- Keep advanced terms out of default reader copy unless the user opens an astrology-detail layer.

## Voice Direction

Relationship copy should be direct, concrete, and useful.

Preferred structure:

1. Name the lived dynamic.
2. Explain the astrological reason.
3. Name the gift and the pressure point.
4. Give one useful thing to notice or do.

Avoid:

- fate claims
- soulmate certainty
- karmic obligation
- fixed compatibility verdicts
- vague spiritualized language
- copy that sounds like backend table joins
