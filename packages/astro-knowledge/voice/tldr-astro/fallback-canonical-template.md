# Canonical Planet-in-Sign Fallback Template — OWNER APPROVED, owner text verbatim (Jul 31)

This supersedes all earlier fallback template descriptions. The template, section rules, and assembly rules below are the owner's, verbatim. The key correction: the fallback should not contain every valid sentence the system has. It should select the smallest set of sentences needed to make one coherent point.

---

```text
# {Planet} in {Sign}

{{entryDate}} to {{exitDate}}

{Planet} moves into {Sign} on {{entryDate}}, bringing more attention to {one concrete area of behavior or daily life}. {Give two or three ordinary examples of what people may notice changing, choosing, starting, postponing, discussing, or reacting to.}

The central tension is {one clear tension created by this placement}. {Show the useful expression through one or two behaviors.} {Show how the same behavior creates a cost when pushed too far.} Keep both sides tied to the same theme.

This period is useful for {plain-language resolution}. {Name two or three specific choices people can make, such as finishing, sharing, asking, deciding, leading, revising, or making time for something.} Continue the point established in the opening. Do not redefine the planet or sign.

{{aspectInsert}}

Before {{exitDate}}, {name one concrete choice that follows from the central tension and can realistically be completed during the transit}.

## Try this

{One action that can be completed this week.}

{One action that belongs specifically to this planet-in-sign placement.}

{Optional third action.}
```

## Section rules

### Fact line

```text
{{entryDate}} to {{exitDate}}
```

Use dates and placement only. Do not add: a transit definition, a subtitle, a slogan, a second explanation of the dates.

### Lived opening

```text
{Planet} moves into {Sign} on {{entryDate}}, bringing more attention to {concrete area}.
```

The opening should show what people may notice.

Good:

```text
A project gets shared.
Someone asks for credit.
A celebration becomes harder to postpone.
```

Avoid:

```text
This sign's priorities move onto the calendar.
The energy becomes visible and unmasked.
This placement activates confidence.
```

Use one paragraph. Do not define the planet or sign.

### Central tension

Choose one tension only. Examples:

```text
Recognition versus letting attention decide what matters.
Care versus taking over because helping feels safer than asking.
Speed versus acting before the facts arrive.
Order versus turning every routine into another standard to fail.
```

The paragraph should show how the same strength can create its own problem. Example:

```text
Wanting the work to be seen can help someone finally share it. The same need for recognition can make another person's praise feel like competition or make early attention seem more important than finishing the work.
```

Do not moralize the sign. Do not create separate "light" and "shadow" definitions.

### Practical development

Do not automatically begin with:

```text
This period rewards...
```

Use natural alternatives:

```text
This period is useful for...
This month gives you time to...
The work now is...
This is a good period to...
You may get further by...
```

Vary the sentence according to the placement. The practical paragraph must continue the central tension. For example, if the tension is recognition versus applause, the actions should involve: sharing work, giving credit, asking clearly, finishing something visible.

Do not suddenly introduce: healing, childhood, purpose, reinvention, relationships, money, unless those subjects already belong to the approved placement meaning.

### Major aspect insert

Only render this section when an approved major aspect is active during the planet-in-sign period.

Use:

```text
## {Planet} {aspect} {Other Planet}

On {{exactDate}}, {Planet} forms a {aspect} with {Other Planet} in {otherSign}.

{Opportunity paragraph: show how the aspect may increase, support, interrupt, test, or complicate the central theme through ordinary behavior.}

{Check paragraph: name the likely overreach, mistake, delay, disagreement, or cost. Keep the outcome conditional and give one practical check when the surface allows advice.}
```

The aspect insert must continue the page's central conflict. For example:

```text
Sun in Leo central tension:
Recognition versus letting attention decide what matters.

Sun conjunct Jupiter insert:
A request may receive more attention.
The plan may grow.
Early encouragement may lead to a larger promise than the schedule can support.
```

Do not let the insert restart with a generic definition of Jupiter, the Sun, or Leo. Do not use slogans such as:

```text
Confidence opens doors.
The wins land larger.
Enthusiasm writes checks execution cannot cash.
```

Name the request, deadline, cost, audience, or responsibility instead.

### Practical close

Use one sentence. It should name one realistic choice:

```text
Before August 23, put one piece of work you are proud of where other people can see it.
```

Do not use a quote-style ending such as:

```text
Vitality follows honesty.
The spotlight reveals the truth.
Your next chapter begins now.
```

The date may appear here for the second and final time.

### Try this

Use two actions by default. Add a third only when it contributes something different.

Each action must: be possible this week, belong specifically to this placement, use an ordinary verb, avoid repeating the practical close.

Good:

```text
Put one finished piece of work where other people can see it.
Give someone clear credit for what they contributed.
Ask directly for the support you need.
```

Weak:

```text
Step into your power.
Align with your authentic self.
Embrace greater visibility.
```

## Assembly rules for Codex and Claude

```text
Render one continuous editorial unit.

Required slots:
1. fact line
2. lived opening
3. central tension
4. practical development
5. practical close

Conditional slots:
6. one or more approved active aspect inserts
7. Try this actions

Do not concatenate every available copy module.

Select one opening, one tension, one development paragraph, and one close.

Each later section must continue the same central tension established in the opening.

Do not render:
- a generic planet definition
- a separate sign definition
- a generic identity hook
- a separate light-versus-shadow module
- a personalized claim without natal data
- a repeated date summary
- a quote-style closing line

Do not claim:
- the reader has been postponing one specific decision
- the reader is living on autopilot
- an old version of the reader needs updating
- a relationship, job, or identity is ending

unless supplied chart data or approved source material supports the claim.

Do not use em dashes or en dashes.

Use conditional language for outcomes:
may, can, might, becomes easier, becomes harder, is more likely to.

Use direct language for the placement's established meaning.

Target length:
350 to 550 words when an aspect insert is active.
220 to 350 words without an aspect insert.
```


## Line-level language rules (owner, from the Sun-in-Leo render review)

The Sun-season fallback does not inherit the architecture of the long articles. History, retrograde mechanics, rising-sign sections, timelines, and rituals belong to events that require more explanation. When a fallback reads wrong, the fix is language cleanup, not more structure.

- **No vague nouns.** "A celebration becomes more important" fails: important how? Name it: "A birthday, launch, or personal win becomes harder to treat like an ordinary day."
- **Lists cap at three nouns.** Four nouns in a row sounds templated. "The title, credit, invitation, or opportunity" becomes "the credit or opportunity."
- **No imported judgments.** Do not introduce a stance the paragraph did not establish ("turn every part of your life into a public event" imported a social-media judgment). Stay with the actual tension: "Not every project needs an audience, but the work you want recognized has to leave your desk eventually."
- **Polished-abstract lines get cashed out.** "The opportunity can be real without being unlimited" becomes "The encouraging response may be real. It still does not mean the budget, deadline, or workload can stretch forever."
- **No vague referents.** "Let the opening be encouraging" fails on "opening." Name the thing: "Take the good response seriously, then check the calendar, the cost, and who is doing the work."
- **No unnatural constructions.** "Share it at the size it is now" becomes "Share the part that is ready now." "Finished becoming" is the most inflated failure mode: work does not finish becoming. "People can see what you made before every detail is perfect."
