Venus enters Cancer on July 30, shifting our collective attention to the places we return to for comfort. This is the kind of love that remembers your favorite song, how you take your coffee, and what helps you feel safe when everything feels uncertain.

Venus in Cancer protects what it loves. It wraps its care around the people, places, and memories that feel like home. But there’s a shadow side too: clinging to what's familiar, confusing love with duty, or emotionally manipulating when we feel insecure. Under this transit, we’re all a little more tender. A little more nostalgic. A little more prone to wanting something that feels like home, even if the house is on fire.

Venus in Cancer invites us to center care, softness, and emotional safety. After the fiery confidence of Venus in Leo, this transit reminds us that tenderness is a strength too. If you’re craving deeper intimacy, seeking comfort, or reflecting on what you need in order to feel secure in your relationships, trust that.

Love isn’t the same as comfort. And comfort isn’t always healthy. Sometimes what feels familiar is actually what’s keeping you stuck.

This year, Venus in Cancer unfolds under the influence of Chiron retrograde in Aries, intensifying our emotional sensitivity and making us more aware of the wounds we carry around vulnerability, identity, and self-protection. You may notice that your desire for closeness is tangled with old survival strategies, like shutting down when you feel unseen, or over-giving just to feel secure. These responses aren’t flaws; they’re habits built from pain. But Venus in Cancer offers the emotional safety to start unraveling them. You don’t need to armor up to be loved. You just need the right space to soften.

As Chiron turns retrograde in Aries, Venus in Cancer shines a light on your emotional defense mechanisms and patterns. This combination brings up the tender places where love has felt conditional, or where care was confused with control. Around August 22, when Venus squares Chiron, the ache of past emotional pains and memories might resurface. Old wounds about not being chosen, protected, or emotionally prioritized could rise to the surface, to offer you a chance to respond differently. This is the work of unlearning reactive independence, learning you're not here to go it alone.
