#### Look back to 2006

Every 19 years, the Moon returns to the same degree and sign during the same season, creating what’s known as a Metonic cycle, a pattern that binds time through memory. This Leo New Moon at 2° is an echo of the one that occurred in July 2006.

Back in 2006, the early foundations of digital identity were taking shape. Twitter launched that spring. Facebook was expanding beyond Harvard. Leo, the sign associated with self-expression and visibility, was activated during a time when the internet was beginning to reshape how we presented ourselves to the world. If you were coming of age then, you were learning to build an online version of yourself, one that could be seen, liked, or accepted. If you were already in the workforce, you may have noticed how LinkedIn, launched in 2003, started to influence hiring and professional image. That era marked a shift in how we began to curate our identities online, both personally and professionally.

For artists and creatives, communities like DeviantArt, LiveJournal, and fan forums offered the first real digital canvases. Art was shared raw, imperfect, and alive. There were no algorithms to game, no pressure to brand your process. You were learning how to show your work sometimes before you understood yourself. And for many, that was the first time they felt seen.

But as the internet evolved, so did the rules of visibility. Sharing became performance. You became content. The freedom of early digital expression gave way to timelines, metrics, and pressure to be consistent.

Reflect on 2006. What was beginning then? What online identity were you just starting to claim? What creative expressions were you afraid to own? What mask were you building? And now, what if you let yourself be as awkward, unfiltered, and real as you were the first time you tried to figure out who you were online?

And if you were too young to remember, or weren’t born yet, you’ve still inherited the impact of that time. You’ve grown up in a world shaped by its standards: its aesthetic, its pace, and its focus on visibility. It was the beginning of a culture that encouraged people to show up online.

Now, with this Leo New Moon in 2025, you have a chance to reflect and redefine what visibility means to you. This isn’t about impressing others or perfecting your image. It’s about reconnecting with your creativity and showing up as yourself. Not for content, but for presence and connection.
