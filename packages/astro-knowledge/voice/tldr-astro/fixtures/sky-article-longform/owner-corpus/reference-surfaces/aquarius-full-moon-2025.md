#### Look back to 1957

The last time we had a Full Moon at exactly 17° Aquarius was on August 10, 1957 (Moon at 17°05′ Aquarius). This is a full moon that is returning to a theme that hasn't occurred in nearly seven decades. If you or your ancestors were alive in 1957, this lunation may carry ancestral themes, inherited stories, or family patterns that are now ready to be seen clearly and released.

Just five days after that Full Moon, on August 15, 1957, the Soviet Union launched the R-7 Semyorka. The world’s first intercontinental ballistic missile. It proved the USSR could strike the U.S. directly, marking a dramatic turning point in the Cold War. Two months later, that same missile would launch Sputnik, officially beginning the Space Race. Humanity was looking up at the sky in awe, but also in fear.

Seven weeks later, on September 29, 1957, the Kyshtym nuclear disaster unfolded in secrecy. A chemical explosion at a Soviet plant released radioactive contamination over thousands of square kilometers. It would go unacknowledged by the government, and the rest of the world wouldn’t hear about it until the 1980s. Families living nearby had no idea what they had been exposed to. These events were unfolding in secret, while most people were still being told to "duck and cover."

Meanwhile, in the U.S., American Bandstand made its national TV debut on August 5, 1957, just days before the Full Moon. It brought youth culture, rock and roll, and new forms of expression into American homes. Shifting public norms and giving a generation a way to see themselves reflected. At the same time, the Civil Rights Act of 1957 passed the Senate on August 7, marking the first civil rights legislation since Reconstruction. But progress was met with backlash: Senator Strom Thurmond launched a 24-hour filibuster on August 28, trying to block the bill and preserve segregation.

So while some were dancing in their living rooms, others were fighting for their right to vote, and still others were living with the silent fear of nuclear fallout.

This was the world our ancestors were navigating. They were hopeful. They were terrified. They were told to keep going. And many didn’t get to speak honestly about what they were feeling or fearing.

If your family lived through this time, you may carry some of that in your nervous system. Fears around safety, silences around race or politics, unspoken grief, or pressure to adapt. This Full Moon is an invitation to ask: What are the stories I’ve inherited? What did my family not get to say?

Aquarius reminds us that healing doesn’t happen in isolation. This is generational. It’s societal. This lunation gives us a chance to tell the truth where silence once lived. To name the fear. To choose clarity. And to live differently than the world that raised us.

If you’re feeling afraid right now, especially with everything happening in the world, you’re not alone. And if this Full Moon has brought up fears about war, disaster, or collapse, it’s not because something terrible is destined to happen. It’s because your body remembers what generations before you had to survive.

If fear is coming up, let it point you toward what matters. Let it reveal where you feel unprotected, disconnected, or silenced, and where you have more agency than you thought. Show up for what you care about. Use your voice. You are not too small. You are not too late. You are here, and that matters.
