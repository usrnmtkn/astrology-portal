## Evidence reviewed before drafting

### Workload, money, and measurable pressure

Source: `/Users/mprez/Downloads/8193683540246852503/19_Weekly Horoscopes.pdf`, page 5, Leo and Leo Rising.

> “You might be obsessing over money, performance, or how much time you’re wasting.”

> “The pressure to ‘do more with less’ is real, but so is the stress it causes.”

Borrowed technique: begin with a recognizable work or money consequence rather than a broad emotional label. Name the operational pressure in ordinary language, then show why continuing at the same pace has a cost.

### Unequal responsibility and the cost of taking over

Source: `/Users/mprez/Downloads/8193683540246852503/19_Weekly Horoscopes.pdf`, page 8, Scorpio and Scorpio Rising.

> “You’re picking up the slack again.”

> “Take a step back and ask if the result is worth the drain.”

Borrowed technique: use a short diagnostic sentence followed by a practical decision. The weekly overview should name unequal labor directly and end with a correction the reader can apply to assignments, deadlines, or shared responsibilities.

### Movement first, correction second

Source: `/Users/mprez/Downloads/8193683540246852503/17_Copy of Taurus in Mercury.pdf`, page 1, May 23, 2025.

> “Mars wants to act. Neptune dissolves action.”

> “Try to brute force your way through this and you’ll end up exhausted, not accomplished.”

Borrowed technique: build contrast with compact sentences. Describe an early push to act, then state the concrete consequence of forcing a decision before the facts, workload, or timing are clear.

### Agreements and the price of false harmony

Source: `/Users/mprez/Downloads/8193683540246852503/13_Pisces New Moon.pdf`, page 8, Scorpio and Scorpio Rising.

> “What have you been pouring yourself into? And what has it cost you?”

Borrowed technique: connect relationship and agreement language to an actual cost. For Venus entering Libra, fairness must mean revisiting who supplies the time, money, labor, or compromise, not simply asking for harmony.

## Drafting constraints derived from the evidence

- Keep the week collective. Do not introduce natal houses, signs, or personal chart claims.
- Use the actual calculated event names in chronological order for the Main shifts row.
- Structure the overview as a sequence: early pressure to move or decide; a midweek workload or resource check; a later review of agreements and fairness; a practical correction.
- Make work, money, labor, responsibility, and agreements explicit.
- End with an observable action: revise the plan, reassign the work, renegotiate the agreement, or reduce the promise.
- Do not imitate ornamental phrases from structural reference publications.
- Do not use em dashes, therapy shorthand, vague transformation language, or generic reassurance.
