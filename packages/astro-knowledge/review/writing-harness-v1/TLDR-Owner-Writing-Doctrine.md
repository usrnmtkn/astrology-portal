# Owner writing doctrine (canonical, 2026-08-08) - applies to all TLDR Astro surfaces

Recorded verbatim from the owner. This supersedes and governs every writer prompt, template,
and review standard. The Codex-facing form the owner designated is the final paragraph.

---

The core rule is:
Write the astrology as a recognizable human experience first. Name what is happening, what
someone does because of it, and what it costs or changes. Only then bring in meaning,
perspective, or advice.

Horoscopes: read like something the reader could notice happening in their actual day, week,
or month. Start with the thesis, not the astrology. Then show where it appears: the unanswered
email, the delayed decision, the extra expense, the person asking for more than you have, the
project finally getting attention, the conversation you keep postponing. Give the transit
consequences in work, money, home, body, time, access, recognition, or relationships. Then
give the reader something useful to do with it, arising from the problem just named.
Structure: What is happening -> how it shows up -> why it matters -> what to do with it.
The astrology explains the experience. It should never replace the experience.

Aspects: show what happens when two functions meet. Show the causal chain: Planet A pushes ->
Planet B complicates or supports -> this is what happens in real life. Never keyword-combine.

Synastry: one person does something -> the other responds -> a recognizable pattern develops.
Stay inside the lived relationship dynamic; no coaching lesson tacked on unless the format
calls for it.

Placements: a recurring way someone operates, not an event. The reader learns: what do I tend
to do, notice, need; what happens when the need is frustrated; what does it look like in
ordinary life. No zodiac-character adjective profiles. A known house changes WHERE the pattern
happens, never triggers a textbook paragraph; without birth time the placement must work
houseless.

The voice underneath: concrete without becoming mundane, psychologically accurate without
sounding clinical, warm without becoming therapeutic, astrologically intelligent without
sounding like a textbook, useful without sounding like a life coach. Never make the reader
translate phrases like "issues of control" or "expansive energy" - name the actual issue.
Truth first. Warmth second.

CODEX INSTRUCTION (owner-designated canonical form): Translate every astrological idea into
lived cause and consequence. Begin with the specific human experience, behavior, conflict,
decision, or consequence the astrology describes. Use concrete stakes such as work, money,
home, body, time, access, recognition, and relationships. For aspects, show one force acting
on another. For synastry, show one person doing something and the other reacting. For
placements, describe the recurring behavior and need rather than predicting an event. Add
perspective, warmth, or advice only after the truth has been clearly named. Never make the
reader decode astrology language to understand what is happening.
