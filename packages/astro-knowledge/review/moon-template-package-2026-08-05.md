# Owner package: Moon-sign transit template + Moon template rules (2026-08-05)

Sole authority for this pass. Owner wording throughout. Companion to the Leo season package; the
global look-back rule already covers the Moon (never a look-back).

## 1. Moon-sign entry structure (owner, fixed)

1. Dates and immediate mood (collective change established once).
2. What the reader may notice in ordinary reactions and decisions.
3. What the sign supports.
4. The behavior that distorts it.
5. The exact aspect (engine-fact-gated; renders only when the engine supplies the aspect and date).
6. One brief reminder that the mood is temporary.
7. Concrete actions.

## 2. Moon template rules (owner)

- Collective mood: the transit affects the atmosphere without giving everyone the same reaction.
  Describe effects (answers take longer, plans change less), not uniform inner states.
- The mood is established ONCE. No re-introduction in the close; opening and closing must not be
  the same paragraph.
- Dates appear at the header and in one closing reminder only. Never four times.
- No generic Moon explainer inside sign entries. The "mood nobody announced" hook is retired
  (owner: it sounds like a universal content hook, not her Moon writing). Her writing does not
  pause to explain the Moon as a concept.
- No keyword-stack adjectives ("unhurried, deliberate, grounded" as a list is not a sentence).
- No poetic-sensory register: skin, hands, sunlight, sweaters, breathing with the world, ground
  conversations. Keep food, rooms, routines, work - recognizable daily life.
- No astrology cliches: "comfort becomes a cage", "a different sky". Name what the person does
  instead ("keeping the routine matters more than admitting it no longer works"; "the mood has
  moved on").
- Sign-word repetition capped: slow, grounded, comfort, anchored appear only where behavior cannot
  carry the meaning.
- The distortion warning is the entry's purpose and stays: a temporary mood is not proof the plan
  is wrong ("A delayed answer, slower day, or stronger need for comfort is not proof that the
  entire plan is wrong.").
- Aspect paragraphs name what someone may actually do (a larger promise, purchase, or plan), not
  inflating abstractions.

Plans are less likely to change at the last minute. A feeling may need time to become clear before
anyone is ready to talk about it.

may want a familiar meal, a quieter room, or enough uninterrupted time to finish what is already in
front of us. Work that produces a visible result can feel more satisfying than another round of
discussion.

This can help us stop reacting to every update and return to what is already working. It can also
make us hold on after the routine has stopped helping. A plan stays in place because changing it
feels inconvenient. A decision gets delayed because the familiar answer feels easier than the honest
one. Patience supports a better choice. Refusing to adjust keeps the same problem going.
