# Calendar exact-aspect collective register contract

This packet rewrites the current 379 exact Sky/Calendar aspect passages for the Calendar `current_sky` surface. It is an editorial candidate package only. It does not change serving copy.

## Scope

Calendar aspects describe a collective condition in the current sky. They do not have natal houses, natal contacts, or another personal chart anchor that would justify claiming a specific reader event, history, motive, relationship dynamic, or emotional state.

The personalized `You` transit surface remains a different register and can make direct second-person claims when the chart facts support them.

## Authoring rule

Each Calendar exact-aspect passage should move through this sequence:

1. **Collective thesis.** Lead with a recognizable condition, behavior, consequence, or tension that the aspect can plausibly describe across many lives.
2. **Astrology mechanism.** Name the two bodies and the aspect in ordinary language. Explain what combines, supports, opposes, or creates friction.
3. **Complication.** Name the common mistake, distortion, excess, or tradeoff the condition can produce.
4. **Practical distinction.** End with the thing that helps the reader understand the moment more clearly without claiming a personal outcome.

## Voice rules for this batch

- Keep the collective condition primary. Calendar is not a natalized surface.
- Second person is optional, not required. Use it only when turning toward the reader makes the passage warmer, clearer, or easier to recognize.
- Keep summaries collective by default. A reader-address turn belongs more naturally in the body after the collective condition and aspect mechanism are established.
- Use second person for **possibility, observation, or reflection**, not for unsupported personal facts. Good forms include `you may notice`, `you might find`, `if you catch yourself`, and `if this is showing up for you`.
- Do not use second person to invent a personal history, motive, argument, relationship, decision, insecurity, emotional state, or event. Calendar should not say `you are arguing`, `you have been ignored`, `your relationship is changing`, or `you know the right move` unless a chart-aware personal surface supports that claim.
- Do not add second person by quota or mechanically. Many passages should remain fully collective.
- Avoid direct commands whose authority depends on pretending the aspect is personal. A conditional or observational reader turn is preferred.
- Use concrete nouns and ordinary stakes: time, money, work, access, responsibility, relationships, home, deadlines, rules, recognition, support, and choice when the astrology supports them.
- Use soft certainty. Prefer `can`, `may`, and conditional descriptions when a manifestation is not guaranteed.
- Do not flatten the writing into detached textbook language. The copy should still sound human and useful.
- Do not replace collective writing with generic `people may feel` scaffolding when a more specific subject is available.
- No em dashes.
- Do not use `whether`.
- Follow the repository voice, literal-language, banned-pattern, and argument-development standards.

## Core distinction

Calendar answers: **What kind of moment is moving through the collective sky?**

Personal transit copy answers: **Why does this moment matter specifically in this chart?**

A Calendar passage can speak directly to the reader without pretending to know what is literally happening to them. The word `you` is not the problem; unsupported personalization is.
