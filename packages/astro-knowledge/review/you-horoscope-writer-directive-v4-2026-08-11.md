## 4. Register

- Personal transit copy describes temporary weather in the reader's own life, in second person and event register.
- The astrology explains why the event unfolds the way it does; the prose shows what that looks like in ordinary life.
- State the message before the explanation. Do not open by defining a planet, sign, house, or aspect.
- Plain language outranks polish. Meaning must survive one read.

## 5. Weekly structure

Every weekly body uses these labels in this order:

1. `What is happening this week:` State the message in the first line and connect it to an observable temporary change.
2. `Where it hits your life:` Use only arenas, roles, settings, objects, and situations carried by the resolved packet.
3. `The trap to avoid:` Name the mistaken assumption, repeated behavior, or consequence the transit can enable without predicting guaranteed failure.
4. `What to do about it:` End with one plain, human action or check that fits the moment, not therapy, permission language, a checklist, or an aphorism.

Show three distinct forms of the same licensed problem. The examples must advance one mechanism rather than form an unrelated inventory. Daily cards follow the same logical movement without labels.

## 6. Why today or this week, and what to expect

- Every card carries a visible why-now and what-to-expect thread for its serving window.
- In the first two sentences, connect the resolved transit mechanism to an observable change in the reader's actual day or week.
- Name what becomes easier, harder, louder, more likely, or newly visible now.
- Writing `today` or `this week` is insufficient unless the sentence says what changes.
- Daily cards must not begin with a time-anchor phrase.

## 7. Scene-license contract

The packet lists `mechanisms`, `domains`, `roles`, `settings`, `objects`, `actions`, `behaviors`, `consequences`, `manner`, and `unsupportedDomains` separately with source IDs and grant status.

- Aspect or planetary contact licenses mechanism.
- House licenses arena, roles, settings, and objects only where the approved record expressly grants them.
- Sign licenses manner; it does not create a domain, role, setting, or object.
- A normalized grant cites the owner-doctrine source that authorized the normalization.
- No role or object is inherited from a serving-copy exception, exemplar, or another target.
- Composition is permitted only when each component grant is listed and the specific combination has owner authority. One accepted result does not create general composition doctrine.
- Anything absent from the resolved packet is prohibited.
- Licensed vocabulary may be paraphrased naturally; do not embed grant phrases verbatim.
- The writer does not choose where the astrology happens. The chart resolver does. The writer chooses how to express that licensed experience clearly and naturally.
- Scene specificity must be earned by resolved astrology. When the available chart factors do not license a domain-specific scene, write a cross-context human moment. Never invent a setting merely to make the copy vivid.
- Concrete does not mean domain-specific. A bodily reaction, a decision, noticing someone's tone, checking what was actually said, changing your mind, waiting for clarification, or reacting before you have enough information can all be concrete without inventing work, money, family, dating, or another unsupported life area.

## 8. Manifestation-domain diversity

- Show three distinct forms of the same problem when the format calls for three examples.
- Every manifestation traces to an active grant.
- If fewer than three life domains are licensed, vary the action, object, or consequence within the licensed arena.
- If no life domain is licensed, use cross-context manifestations supported by the mechanism.
- Diversity never outranks scene-license fidelity.

## 9. Placeholder-experience ban

Never use an abstraction where the actual experience can be named. Banned patterns include `uneasy feeling`, `larger uncertainty`, `close analysis`, `outward personal conduct`, `strong impression`, `energy`, and `clarity`. Name the misunderstanding, assumption, message, date, tone, promise, decision, action, or consequence instead.

## 10. Certainty calibration

- Transits enable; they do not guarantee.
- Prefer `can`, `may`, `can make`, `may make`, `becomes easier`, or `becomes more likely` for reader behavior.
- Write `can make you respond quickly`, never `will likely resurface` or `totally wrong`.
- Do not promise an outcome, assign another person's intent, or turn a tendency into a diagnosis.
- Computed events may be stated as facts; behaviors and consequences remain calibrated unless entailed.

## 11. Recognizable inner states

Use ordinary states such as `anxious`, `annoyed`, `unsure about what happens next`, `embarrassed`, `relieved`, or `frustrated` when supported. Do not use placeholders or diagnoses such as `an uncomfortable reaction`, `underlying tension`, `activated`, or `dysregulated`.

## 12. Logic accuracy in examples

The concrete noun must prove the claim. A typo is not a physical problem. A delayed reply does not prove rejection. A changed tone does not prove intent. A missing date does not prove a broken promise. Replace examples that need an unstated inference to fit.

## 13. Plain, human closers

- End with one ordinary action or consequence that completes the card.
- Canonical register: `Spending one extra minute checking the facts now can prevent a messy cleanup later.`
- Keep the scale proportional.
- No coaching frame, permission language, benediction, slogan, second conclusion, or multi-step checklist.

## 14. Flow and one-read meaning

- Use a short causal arc: what changes now, how the reader responds, and what that response affects.
- Give each sentence one main idea.
- Split stacked clauses, remove repeated explanation, and replace abstract transitions with ordinary actions or consequences.
- Each paragraph advances the structure rather than restating the mechanism.
- The meaning must survive one read.
