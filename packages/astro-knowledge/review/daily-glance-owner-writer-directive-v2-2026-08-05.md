## The voice target (owner's words)

"direct truth + lived consequence + humane understanding. Not: poetic opening + therapeutic reassurance + generic instruction."

Marie is the foundation: the writing names the event, power dynamic, cost, contradiction, or consequence in ordinary language first. Chani contributes warmth AFTER the situation is clear; the reader is not shamed for the coping mechanism. Never imitation of another writer's phrasing; the target is the owner's voice with more warmth and breathing room.

## The seven diagnosed failures (each is now a rule)

- D2-1 TRUTH BEFORE SCENE: do not open with a manufactured scene ("You ask for a ride...", "You open the calendar..."). Open closer to the conclusion ("Their good mood starts spending your time."). The lived example supports the point; it must not delay it.
- D2-2 STOP AT THE STRONGEST LINE: when a line has a pulse ("You send something warm, receive no reply, and suddenly feel hard to like"), everything after it must deepen or change understanding, never interpret it for the reader.
- D2-3 NOT EVERY CARD IS A COACHING MODULE: the ending completes the thought, not a template. Endings may be a practical move, a permission, a consequence, or a recognition line ("No reply is not the same as rejection, even when your body reacts as though the answer has already arrived."). An instruction is optional.
- D2-4 PERMISSION IS NOT INSIGHT: "You don't have to... / You are not weak for... / That does not make you..." without a specific truth underneath is generic reassurance. Identify what happened and why the reader started doubting themselves; often no permission line is then needed. (Owner example: "Help stops feeling generous when accepting it makes you responsible for coordinating everyone who offered.")
- D2-5 LIVED MEANS STAKES, NOT PROPS: concrete writing is not a candle, grocery run, cafe, calendar, and group chat per batch. Lived = recognizable stakes: losing income, splitting bills, being expected to repair every relationship, preserving an arrangement already broken, being called difficult because a boundary inconveniences someone, needing support and being too proud to ask, a composed exterior that costs too much. A domestic scene works only when its consequence matters.
- D2-6 NO BALANCED AI SENTENCES: do not build paragraphs from symmetrical opposites ("This feels good, but it may become too much" / "You can do X without doing Y" / "The issue is not A. It is B."). The owner's rhythm: a longer sentence carries full context, then a blunt correction ("You agreed before checking the work already waiting for you. Now their spontaneous afternoon is becoming your late night. Their excitement does not change the deadline.").
- D2-7 SOFT CERTAINTY, NOT HEDGING: make the chart-supported claim clearly; qualify only where the lived outcome genuinely varies. Never stack might/could/possible until the paragraph refuses to say anything.

## The new structure

- Headline CONTAINS the interpretation, not the subject. (Flat: "Moving one errand does not require a new routine." Sharp: "The old routine is costing more time than changing it would." Flat: "You can accept help before the struggle is over." Sharp: "Being seen stuck may feel worse than staying stuck.")
- Body does three jobs, order flexible, not all always required: (1) name what happens through a recognizable action, decision, exchange, expense, deadline, silence, responsibility, or reaction; (2) name what it means or costs - who loses time, who gains control, what becomes harder, what fear takes over; (3) leave one clear turn - move, permission, consequence, or a line that changes how the reader sees the situation.

## S3 style addendum (extracted from the owner's three authored pairs, 2026-08-05, at her request: "update the writing style to match what i just gave you")

- S3-1: headlines are plain conversational truths carrying the interpretation; the situation, not the reader, is usually the subject.
- S3-2: bodies run in plain present indicative; hedges live in headlines if anywhere; no "may" for what the reader is plainly doing or feeling.
- S3-3: lists escalate and can carry humor; a list is a crescendo, not an inventory.
- S3-4: timing details make it lived ("within ten minutes", "before you have even settled the first change").
- S3-5: contrast is allowed when it corrects a misreading of the cost; still banned as mere balance.
- S3-6: the close may be one compound instruction of two or three coordinated moves ending on a release, or instruction followed by a recognition line.
- S3-7: recognition lines can strip false authority from the trigger ("A slow reply does not get to decide what you are worth.").
- FORMAT AMENDMENT (owner-demonstrated three times): body gate widens to 40-90 words, 3-6 sentences, instruction position flexible; a compound close counts as one instruction. The saturn pair's ~90 words is the new outer bound, resolving the earlier cap flag.

## Sentence-level standard (verbatim lists)

Contain: concrete nouns and verbs; direct cause and consequence; one strong line rather than five acceptable lines; varied sentence length; emotional accuracy without therapy language; practical stakes without reducing every problem to productivity; warmth that does not excuse the behavior described; enough astrology underneath to make the scene specific to the transit.

Avoid: "You don't have to..." as recurring structure; "This is an invitation to..."; "For the next few hours..." inserted mechanically; "You may notice a feeling of..."; "There could be something..."; "Make sure to..."; "You already know how to do it"; "Do it, even if you struggle"; abstract stacks ("individuality, rebellion, and creativity"); vague subconscious language; a fictional scene at the beginning of every card; a practical instruction at the end of every card; explaining the metaphor after it has landed.
