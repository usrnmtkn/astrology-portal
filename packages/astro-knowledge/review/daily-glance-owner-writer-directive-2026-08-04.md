## The owner's closing instruction (packet prompt core, verbatim)

Draft from the lived moment, not from the astrological concept. Name what the reader is doing, what happens next, and what the behavior costs them. Replace abstract summaries with ordinary decisions, errands, messages, work, plans, money, time, and relationships. Use one concrete scene instead of listing every possible manifestation. Limit hedging. Let the headline make a clear claim in language someone would actually say. Reassurance must answer the specific fear created by the scene, not offer generic emotional permission. End with one action that directly repairs or clarifies the moment described. Vary the sentence rhythm, including at least one short, blunt sentence. Before finalizing, reject any line that could fit almost any transit or sounds like therapy, corporate training, or generic productivity advice.

## The nine steps (condensed; owner wording preserved where quoted)

1. Find the lived moment before drafting: what is the person doing, what happens immediately before the pattern, what do they realize afterward, what does the choice cost them.
2. Headlines are something a person would actually say: a recognizable contradiction, a consequence, a blunt truth, or a moment already in motion. Reject lesson/category/definition/HR-slide/summary headlines.
3. One concrete scene, not category stacks. "One short list can work, but repeated category stacks make the copy feel generated." Choose the strongest scene; keep language broad enough to fit elsewhere without naming every possibility.
4. Name the cost: every card answers "What becomes harder because of this pattern?" Without the consequence the paragraph is observational and flat.
5. Reduce hedging before changing vocabulary: one "may" or "can" is usually enough; after that, describe the behavior directly. The disease is refusing to take a position, not the word itself.
6. Reassurance must be specific: permission only when it answers the exact shame or fear the scene creates ("Changing the plan does not make you unreliable"; "Wanting more does not make your current life a failure"). Generic permission lines are rejected.
7. The instruction repairs the exact moment, never general wellness advice ("Decide what you want the next ten minutes to accomplish", not "Take three breaths").
8. Vary sentence length: sharp headline; scene sentence; longer pattern/consequence sentence; one blunt turn; one concrete action.
9. Three final tests: SPECIFICITY (could this appear under five unrelated transits? then too generic), MORNING-READ (understandable half-awake), SCREENSHOT (one sentence worth saving because it names something the reader has experienced but not said this clearly).

## Additional owner rulings embedded in the same notes

- Permission comes AFTER the reader recognizes themselves: name the behavior, show the consequence, then reframe. Never lead with reassurance.
- The strongest scenes are ordinary and current (the closed tab, the half-done errands, the hour kept for yourself).
- "Suppress", "full accounting" flagged as clinical/formal; replace with plain speech ("pushing it down", "explaining every detail").
