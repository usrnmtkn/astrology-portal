# Owner horoscope template: FORECAST-FIRST, ASTROLOGY-ANCHORED (canonical, 2026-08-08)

Recorded from the owner's full ruling, which supersedes the V4 synthesis and the doctrine-first
application for horoscopes. Movement: lived forecast -> astrology explained -> recognizable
examples -> possible complication -> useful response.

1. OPEN WITH THE LIVED FORECAST: something the reader may notice, want, care about, or become
   more sensitive to. Never assume a conflict already exists. Constructions: "You may
   notice...", "You may feel more drawn to...", "This season can bring more attention to...",
   "You may care more than usual about...", "It may become easier to..."
2. EXPLAIN THE ASTROLOGY IMMEDIATELY AFTER (sentence two or three), in plain language:
   planet = what gets emphasized; sign = how it wants to operate; house = where the reader
   experiences it. Teach enough that the forecast belongs to this transit; never a lesson.
3. TWO OR THREE ORDINARY MANIFESTATIONS, possibility language. Specific does not have to mean
   scripted; give possibilities with room for recognition, not a scene the reader may not be
   living.
4. NAME THE COMPLICATION WITHOUT INDICTMENT. Retire the mechanical "The cost is..." line.
   Use: "The same desire can also...", "Where this gets complicated is...", "This can become
   frustrating if...", "The harder side of this season is..." Keep consequences proportionate.
5. END WITH THE USEFUL MOVE, connected to the named pattern. No commandments, no forced
   aphorism; the beautiful closer must be earned, not required.

ACCESSIBILITY GOVERNOR (owner): Do not make specificity depend on certainty. Prefer "You may
notice yourself caring more about who gets credit." Avoid "You are tired of watching someone

CODEX FORMULA (owner verbatim): Write each horoscope as a forecast, not a diagnosis. Open with
a recognizable desire, shift, concern, opportunity, or area of increased attention that the
reader may notice during the transit. Do not assume that a conflict, insecurity, resentment,
or problem is already happening. Name the astrology within the next one or two sentences and
briefly explain it in plain language. The planet describes what function is being emphasized,
the sign describes how that function is behaving, and the house describes where it is showing
up in ordinary life. Teach enough astrology that the reader understands why the forecast
belongs to this transit, but do not turn the paragraph into an astrology lesson. Then give two
or three concrete ways the transit may appear in work, money, home, body, time, creativity,
relationships, recognition, communication, or everyday decisions. Use possibility language
such as "may," "might," "can," and "you may notice" so that specificity creates recognition
rather than accusation. Name one complication or excess that belongs specifically to the
astrology. Keep it proportionate and behavioral. Do not invent a crisis, assign motives,
diagnose the reader, or use a dramatic "cost" line simply to make the copy hit harder. End
with one useful response to the actual pattern described. Advice should be practical and
connected to the transit. Warmth or permission can follow, but do not force an aphorism,
motivational slogan, or therapeutic reassurance into every ending. Overall movement: lived
forecast -> astrology explained -> recognizable examples -> possible complication -> useful
response. The paragraph should feel observant rather than edgy, intelligent rather than
academic, and specific without telling the reader a story they may not be living.

COLD RENDERED PROSE RULE (owner verbatim, 2026-08-11, written after the V7 Mercury masters
failed her cold read; BLOCKING for all reader-facing copy):

Read the copy cold, rendered, and line by line as prose. Judge the final text exactly as a
reader would encounter it in the product. Do not use the prompt, source notes, astrology
logic, intended meaning, or drafting context to help the writing make sense.

Every sentence must: make sense on the first read; flow naturally from the sentence before
it; lead naturally into the sentence after it; use normal, everyday language; sound like
something a human writer would actually say; state the intended meaning directly enough
that the reader does not have to decode it.

Prefer the ordinary word when it is more natural. Use work instead of labor unless labor is
literally the subject. Do not choose a more formal, abstract, clever, or literary word just
because it sounds elevated.

A sentence fails if the judge has to stop and ask what it means, mentally translate it into
simpler English, infer a missing connection, or rely on knowledge of the astrology to
understand the prose.

Also judge the paragraph as a whole. Flag: abrupt jumps between ideas; sentences that
technically make sense alone but do not connect; vague referents such as it, this, that,
the change when the reader may not know what they refer to; report-heavy transitions;
clever compression; abstract summaries where the actual behavior could be named; repeated
setup or explanation; sentences that sound assembled rather than written; unnecessarily
formal vocabulary; a strong sentence followed by another sentence that explains the same
point again.

Cold-read test: after drafting, ignore what the writer intended and read only the rendered
copy. If any line produces "Wait, what does that mean?", "Why are we suddenly talking about
this?", or "A normal person would say this more simply," the line is not approved.

Final judge instruction: Do not reward a sentence for being astrologically correct if it is
awkward prose. Correct astrology expressed in unnatural language still fails the writing
judge.

## EDITORIAL REASONER (owner-issued 2026-08-08, verbatim; use for both Codex and Claude)

This instruction set sits alongside the writing style and the judge; every draft and every
edit pass runs through it.

Act as an editorial reasoner for TLDR Astro. Do not rewrite by instinct. Reason from the
astrology first, preserve the interpretation, then refine surgically at the sentence level.

Use Marie Satori's owner-authored corpus and explicit owner edits as the voice authority. Do
not treat assistant-generated copy as a voice benchmark unless that exact wording has been
explicitly approved.

For every sentence, work in this order:

1. Astrological logic: Identify exactly what the planet, sign, house, aspect, or relationship
   dynamic is supposed to mean. The astrology must produce one coherent behavior, forecast, or
   cause-and-consequence pattern, not a stack of keywords.
2. Lived meaning: Translate the astrology into something observable. Prefer messages, rates,
   bills, rooms, schedules, appointments, projects, titles, invitations, accounts, trips,
   sleep, workload, access, credit, plans, responsibilities, and specific relationship
   behavior.
3. No vagueness: Ask, "Could the reader reasonably ask what this actually means?" If yes,
   rewrite. Abstract words such as recognition, support, visibility, belonging, confidence,
   closure, freedom, value, connection, or growth must be immediately shown through behavior
   or stakes.
4. Word choice: Choose the most observable noun and the verb that shows the action. Prefer
   ordinary spoken English over technically correct but unnatural phrasing. Reject
   generated-sounding phrases such as "do a role," "support sitting unspoken," or vague
   pronouns with unclear referents.
5. Cause and consequence: Show what changes, what someone notices or does, and what happens
   because of it. In synastry, show what one person does and how the other responds.
6. Specific, not scripted: Give two or three recognizable possibilities. Do not invent one
   dramatic scene or assume a crisis the reader may not be living.
7. Certainty: Preserve the correct level of certainty. Horoscopes forecast possibilities with
   may, might, or can. Do not weaken statements that define the astrology itself.
8. Emotional temperature: Do not add intensity for impact. Use the mildest word that is still
   accurate. Do not soften precise writing into therapy language or generic reassurance
   either.
9. Marie voice: Keep direct thesis statements, concrete stakes, natural rhythm, controlled
   metaphor, useful contrast, occasional rhetorical questions, and short declarative sentences
   when earned. Observation earns the edge.
10. Repetition: Review the whole batch. Catch repeated nouns, repeated meanings, repeated
    sentence bridges, and visible template syntax such as "bringing more attention," "Leo
    can," or identical openings. Vary syntax only when the replacement is equally clear.
11. Sentence progression: Each sentence must add something new. The astrology anchor should
    explain the forecast, not paraphrase the opening in astrology language.
12. Closer: The ending should compress the logic already established and give one useful
    response. Do not introduce a new lesson, diagnosis, slogan, or emotional theme at the end.
13. Natural-language pass: Read the sentence aloud. Fix awkward noun stacks, stiff
    constructions, repeated clause shapes, unnatural prepositions, and anything a person would
    understand but would not actually say.
14. Edit discipline: Do not substitute synonyms recreationally. Every change must have a
    reason: greater accuracy, specificity, naturalness, grammatical correctness, better
    cadence, less repetition, or clearer cause and consequence. If the sentence already
    passes, leave it alone.

Before accepting any change, be able to explain:

* What ambiguity did this remove?
* What behavior did this make easier to picture?
* What astrological distinction did this preserve or sharpen?
* Did it change the certainty, emotional temperature, or scope?
* Does it sound like language Marie actually uses?

Governance: Never label generated or refined wording as owner-authored, owner-approved,
exact, settled, or locked until the owner explicitly approves that exact wording.

Stop rule: Once the structure, astrology, specificity, and natural language are correct, stop
broad rewriting. Switch to surgical edits and do not reopen strong sentences without a
concrete reason.
