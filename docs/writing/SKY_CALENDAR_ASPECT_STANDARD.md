# TLDR Astro: Planetary Aspect Writing Standard
### Sky and Calendar | Editorial Voice and Astrological Interpretation

## 1. Purpose

Write planetary aspect interpretations that help readers understand what's happening in the sky, how the planets interact, and how that interaction might show up in everyday life.

The writing should feel like a knowledgeable astrologer talking directly to the reader. It should be insightful, approachable, psychologically perceptive, and occasionally funny. Use everyday English, recognizable human experiences, and observations that reveal something meaningful about how people behave.

Readers should come away understanding what the aspect means without having to translate astrological language or guess what the writer is trying to say.

**The planets describe what's involved. The aspect describes how those influences interact. The interpretation explains what that might mean for our lives.**

These are collective sky-to-sky transit interpretations, shared by everyone. They are not personalized natal readings.

## 2. The Five Major Aspects

The type of aspect must shape the entire interpretation, including its possible events, emotional experience, challenges, and opportunities.

### ☌ Conjunction: Two Influences Come Together

A conjunction brings two planetary principles together, strengthening their interaction and beginning a new phase in their relationship.

The planets influence each other's expression. Their themes may become more noticeable, more closely connected, or harder to separate.

A conjunction can bring an important development, a new beginning, a turning point, or a situation that brings both planetary concerns into focus.

A conjunction isn't automatically positive or difficult. Its meaning depends on the planets involved, their condition, and any additional verified astrological context.

**Writing direction:** Show what happens when both planetary principles become involved in the same experience. Explain what makes their meeting significant.

Don't describe conjunctions as universally harmonious or assume every conjunction begins a major life chapter.

### ⚹ Sextile: Help, Encouragement, and Opportunity

A sextile is a supportive aspect associated with cooperation, encouragement, and opportunities that can become useful when we engage with them.

Help may arrive through another person, useful information, an invitation, a change in circumstances, or a better way of approaching something.

Sextiles are often less obvious than trines. The opportunity may be available, but the person still has to recognize or pursue it.

**Writing direction:** Describe what becomes easier to explore, pursue, discuss, or accomplish. Include what kind of help or cooperation could make a difference.

A sextile should feel like having something useful available to work with.

Don't turn every sextile into a small breakthrough or guarantee that taking action produces a successful outcome.

### □ Square: Friction That Pushes Something Forward

A square creates tension between two planetary principles that don't cooperate easily.

The tension can become difficult to ignore. Competing needs, demands, beliefs, or responsibilities may require attention, especially when an existing approach is no longer working.

Squares can bring frustration, disagreement, delays, or situations that require a decision. They can also produce creativity, determination, and necessary change.

The challenge is part of the interpretation, but it shouldn't become the entire story.

**Writing direction:** Identify the actual source of friction. Explain why it matters, what might become difficult, and what could change because of it.

A square should give the reader a sense of what is being challenged and why the situation may require effort.

Don't automatically predict an argument, crisis, confrontation, or negative outcome.

### △ Trine: Ease, Opportunity, and Things Coming Together

A trine is associated with cooperation, ease, natural progress, and circumstances that work together with less resistance.

Helpful developments can happen without the usual struggle. An idea may receive support, a conversation might go well, or an opportunity could appear at a useful moment.

Sometimes the effect is subtle. A trine doesn't need to produce a major event to be meaningful.

Unlike a sextile, which often presents an opening that benefits from active participation, a trine describes circumstances that tend to work together more naturally.

**Writing direction:** Show what flows more easily, what becomes possible, and where people might experience support, recognition, understanding, or progress.

Trines should allow room for enjoyment, confidence, good experiences, and things working out.

Don't turn every trine into advice about making the most of an opportunity. Don't invent luck, success, or approval.

### ☍ Opposition: Contrasts, Relationships, and Realization

An opposition brings two planetary principles into direct contrast.

These differences can become visible through relationships, public disagreements, competing priorities, or circumstances that require us to consider more than one point of view.

Oppositions can expose what we've overlooked, what we're projecting onto others, or where two legitimate needs are difficult to reconcile.

They can also bring awareness, important realizations, and opportunities to understand a situation more fully.

**Writing direction:** Develop both sides of the tension. Show how the contrast becomes visible and why it matters.

An opposition should reveal something through comparison, disagreement, or recognition.

Don't automatically make one person right and another wrong. Don't reduce every opposition to a power struggle or a relationship conflict.

## 3. The Planets Must Keep Their Own Meanings

The aspect modifies how the planets interact. It does not erase what either planet represents.

Sun: identity, visibility, authority, purpose, self-expression, and recognition.

Moon: emotional needs, family, care, belonging, comfort, and our immediate reactions.

Mercury: communication, information, learning, decisions, questions, reasoning, and misunderstandings.

Venus: love, attraction, affection, relationships, money, beauty, and what we value.

Mars: action, motivation, anger, competition, courage, conflict, and pursuing what we want.

Jupiter: faith, opportunity, wisdom, education, travel, generosity, moral convictions, and excess.

Saturn: responsibility, limits, authority, commitment, accountability, time, and consequences.

Uranus: independence, change, innovation, experimentation, disruption, and challenging convention.

Neptune: imagination, ideals, spirituality, longing, fantasy, compassion, illusion, and disillusionment.

Pluto: power, control, secrecy, influence, transformation, and the consequences of unequal power.

Chiron: vulnerability, difficult experiences, exclusion, understanding, and the knowledge that can develop through experience.

Black Moon Lilith: autonomy, refusal, shame, rejection, desire, social expectations, exile, and the consequences of refusing to conform.

Lunar Nodes: direction, recurring patterns, unfamiliar experiences, and the relationship between established habits and future development.

For every interpretation, identify the central interaction between the two specific planets or points.

**Do not substitute the same story about boundaries, authority, self-expression, or personal growth for every planetary combination.**

## 4. Writing Voice

Write in a direct, warm, conversational voice with light lyricism, dry humor when appropriate, and psychologically perceptive observations.

Use words people actually say.

A good interpretation may begin with something recognizable about relationships, work, ambition, money, family, curiosity, attraction, or the strange things people do when they're uncomfortable.

The observations should feel earned. The reader should recognize the behavior immediately.

Prefer:

"Some rules stay in place for years because the people stuck following them aren't the ones making the decisions."

Avoid:

"Established structures may experience a shift toward greater accountability and equitable responsibility."

Prefer:

"People have a funny way of calling something weird until it catches on. Then suddenly everyone acts like they thought it was a great idea all along."

Avoid:

"Unconventional choices may find an easier welcome as collective attitudes evolve."

The writing can be intelligent without sounding academic. It can be funny without trying to deliver a punchline. It can be emotionally perceptive without turning into therapy language.

### Language Rules

- Use ordinary, modern English. Favor concrete nouns and verbs over abstract phrases.
- Avoid em dashes, corporate jargon, forced metaphors, therapy clichés, and decorative language.
- Do not use "fair," "fairer," "whether," "step into your power," or "highest self" in reader-facing copy.
- Avoid repeated constructions beginning with "You might," "Someone could," or "This aspect can."
- Don't repeatedly use "creates space," "finds an easier welcome," "supports growth," "brings an opportunity for," or similar generic phrases.
- Avoid vague endings, manufactured wisdom, and automatic lessons.
- Don't use humor just because the reading needs an interesting opening.
- Don't use a planetary definition when the writing has already demonstrated the planet's meaning.
- Don't manufacture childhood experiences, personal histories, motives, or psychological wounds.
- Don't make every interpretation about conflict, injustice, or the reader needing to speak up.

The goal is natural writing with enough substance to make the astrology interesting.

## 5. Collective Astrology With Personal Relevance

These interpretations describe aspects happening in the sky. Everyone shares the same planetary configuration, but people may experience its themes differently.

Each reading should communicate what the aspect means collectively and provide ways the reader could recognize it personally.

Collective developments might involve changing social attitudes, public conversations, institutions, artistic developments, economic concerns, or shared experiences.

Personal developments might involve a conversation, invitation, decision, relationship, creative project, change in circumstances, or a realization.

Use specific examples when they clarify the astrology.

Don't assume everyone is having trouble with their boss, arguing with family, breaking up, or questioning authority.

The collective and personal meanings should flow naturally within the writing. They do not require separate paragraphs or repeated phrases such as "In your personal life."

The astrology should remain the reason the interpretation exists, even when the writing begins with an ordinary human observation.

## 6. Social Context and Meaningful Contrasts

Social observations are part of TLDR Astro's editorial voice, especially when they help explain how planetary themes operate in the world.

Explore the contradiction between what people say they believe and how they behave.

Examples include the difference between valuing independence and accepting another person's independence, encouraging honesty and responding badly to criticism, celebrating creativity and dismissing unfamiliar ideas, or demanding responsibility while refusing to share the work.

Name who benefits from an expectation and who deals with its consequences when that distinction genuinely matters to the aspect.

Don't insert social criticism into every reading. A supportive Venus trine can be about attraction or an enjoyable development. A Jupiter sextile can be about learning, generosity, or an opportunity. Not every planetary aspect needs an institutional critique.

Also preserve the uncomfortable side of human behavior. The person challenging a rule isn't automatically right. The person enforcing it isn't automatically wrong.

**The contrast must emerge from the astrology and the human situation, rather than being invented to make the writing sound profound.**

## 7. Possible Events and Experiences

A horoscope should occasionally describe what might actually happen.

A Mercury aspect could coincide with a meaningful conversation, new information, a published story, or a disagreement.

A Venus aspect might involve attraction, a financial development, a creative opportunity, or an important relationship experience.

A Mars aspect may involve a decision, competition, initiative, anger, or a situation requiring action.

A Jupiter aspect can involve education, travel, encouragement, opportunity, a teacher, or questions about belief.

A Saturn aspect may involve a deadline, agreement, responsibility, restriction, policy, or commitment.

The example must be appropriate to the specific aspect, not simply the planet.

Avoid stacking unrelated possibilities just to demonstrate range. Develop one or two useful examples that actually illuminate the interpretation.

Don't promise that an event will happen, invent a personal transit, or imply that a collective aspect activates a reader's natal planet or house.

## 8. Structure and Length

Each interpretation is stored as **one shared CMS entry** for Sky and Calendar.

Calendar shows a preview that readers can expand. Sky displays the full interpretation.

The opening sentences must contain enough meaning to work as a standalone preview. They should make the aspect understandable without relying on a later paragraph.

Aim for approximately 100–150 words, allowing shorter or longer readings when the astrology requires it.

There is no mandatory paragraph count or sentence formula.

A reading might begin with the aspect, a meaningful observation, a recognizable situation, or an opportunity. Choose the opening that explains this particular interaction most naturally.

Don't force the same sequence of collective meaning, personal example, planetary explanation, and advice across the entire set.

The writing should follow the idea, rather than making the idea fit a predetermined structure.

## 9. Endings

A reading does not need to finish with advice.

It can end with a realization, a possible consequence, an interesting contrast, an opportunity, or a sentence that completes the experience.

If the preceding sentence has already made the point, stop there.

Avoid endings such as:

"Remember to honor your needs."

"Use this energy to create positive change."

"This is an opportunity for growth."

"Make sure you've considered every perspective."

Don't add a conclusion that simply repeats the interpretation in different words.

**End when the reader has enough to understand the aspect.**

## 10. Astrological Accuracy

Use only the verified aspect, planets or points, signs, dates, degrees, and timing supplied by the astrology data.

If the aspect is applying or separating, or is waxing or waning, use that distinction only when supplied and relevant.

Do not introduce a specific sign, natal house, aspect to a birth chart, or retrograde condition without supporting data.

Do not treat a brief Moon aspect as though its influence lasts for months, or a slow-moving outer-planet aspect as a single-day emotional event.

If the same aspect becomes exact more than once, distinguish the recurring contact only when the underlying calculated data supports it.

For Black Moon Lilith, use the configured calculation consistently. Mean and True Black Moon Lilith do not always occupy the same degree or form exact aspects on the same dates.

Astrology describes the symbolic nature and timing of an influence. It does not guarantee a particular external event or another person's response.

## 11. Editorial Quality Check

Before publishing an interpretation, check:

1. **Aspect meaning:** Does the interpretation actually demonstrate a conjunction, sextile, square, trine, or opposition? Could it be swapped with another aspect without changing much? If so, rewrite it.

2. **Planetary meaning:** Are both planets or points meaningfully involved? Does the writing describe their interaction rather than just listing their keywords?

3. **Clarity:** Can an ordinary reader understand the opening without knowing advanced astrology?

4. **Human experience:** Is there a recognizable event, decision, feeling, interaction, or observation that makes the astrology relevant?

5. **Voice:** Would a thoughtful human astrologer naturally say this? Are any phrases vague, formal, awkward, or trying too hard to sound clever?

6. **Originality:** Does the interpretation sound different from other cards? Are the same examples, conclusions, and sentence patterns being repeated?

7. **Balance:** Does it preserve appropriate positive and difficult possibilities without manufacturing problems or guaranteeing outcomes?

8. **Length:** Has the idea been developed fully without repetitive explanations or unnecessary advice?

9. **Collective relevance:** Does the reading work for a general Sky audience without pretending to know the reader's personal chart?

10. **Ending:** Does the final sentence add something meaningful? If not, remove it.

## Final Editorial Principle

Write about the experience first when that produces the clearest interpretation. Introduce the astrology naturally and explain enough for the reader to understand why the experience belongs to this particular aspect.

The writing should have substance, personality, and a clear point of view without becoming a lecture.

**The aspect must change the story.**

A sextile offers help that may be useful. A trine allows things to come together more naturally. A square creates friction that can move a situation forward. An opposition reveals contrasting needs or perspectives. A conjunction brings two influences together and can begin or intensify a shared development.

If those distinctions aren't apparent in the writing, the interpretation isn't finished.

And if the reader has to work to understand what a sentence means, rewrite the sentence.
