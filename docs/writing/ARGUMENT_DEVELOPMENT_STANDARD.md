# Argument-developed interpretation standard

Status: owner-authored and exact-owner-approved register calibration, 2026-08-25.

This standard governs developed natal placement interpretations and any other
long-form reader passage whose subject is a recurring human pattern. It extends
the long-form sentence architecture in
`MARIE_SATORI_LONG_FORM_VOICE_STANDARD.md`; it does not replace surface-specific
registers, fact boundaries, evidence retrieval, or owner review.

## The distinction

Accurate information about a placement is not yet a developed interpretation.
A trait list followed by a problem and advice can contain correct astrology and
still feel flat. The writing must explain how the astrological mechanism becomes
a way of living.

For a developed placement or wound-pattern passage, reason through:

```text
placement mechanism
  -> what experience may have made adaptation necessary
  -> what the person became capable of because of it
  -> when that competence keeps operating after circumstances change
  -> the hidden cost or contradiction
  -> how the house changes where or how the pattern operates
  -> what changes in behavior and operating assumptions
```

These are semantic movements, not paragraph slots. Do not announce them, assign
one sentence to each, or repeat the same construction across placements. Do not
force this exact history-and-adaptation arc onto a short card, a current forecast,
or a surface whose governed facts do not support personal history.

## Required reasoning

### 1. Begin with the mechanism, not the label

Identify the psychological or behavioral distinction created by the exact
planet or point and sign. Do not stop at a topic such as money, stability,
self-worth, healing, communication, or trust. Name the distinction that makes
the pattern internally coherent.

### 2. Show adaptation before pathology

When the astrology and surface support developmental interpretation, explain
what the person may have learned to do and why that response once made sense.
Treat resourcefulness, privacy, vigilance, competence, restraint, or
self-sufficiency as an adaptation before naming its cost. Do not invent a
specific childhood event, diagnosis, trauma, or motive. Origins remain
possibilities unless governed evidence establishes more.

### 3. Let competence become the hinge

The strongest interpretation often turns where a useful skill continues after
the original conditions have changed. The problem is not that the person learned
the wrong thing. The problem is that an intelligent response can keep governing
choices where it is no longer necessary. Show that change through decisions and
consequences rather than diagnostic shorthand.

### 4. Find the contradiction underneath the behavior

The contradiction must belong to the placement. It should explain why a costly
pattern can still feel reasonable or safer than its alternative. It is not a
generic shadow, a dramatic cost line, or a slogan added for impact.

### 5. Make the house alter the mechanism

A house is not an appended topic paragraph. It changes where the pattern runs,
how visible it is, what choices it influences, or why it is difficult to
recognize. The sign and house must produce one integrated argument. Removing the
house paragraph should materially change the interpretation when reliable birth
time is available.

### 6. End with changed operation, not generic advice

Healing, maturity, or development should change what the person notices, assumes,
chooses, asks for, accepts, or stops preparing for. Do not resolve the passage
with confidence, empowerment, self-acceptance, or another abstract destination.
The ending must follow from the contradiction already developed.

## Surface application

- **Full natal placement and Chiron detail:** use the complete causal movement
  when the governed meaning supports it. Do not invent biography.
- **Transits, Sky placements, Calendar, and horoscopes:** preserve causal depth,
  but write a forecast rather than a retrospective diagnosis. Show the present
  pressure, the familiar response it may activate, when that response becomes
  costly, and the useful behavioral distinction.
- **Synastry and relationship writing:** keep directionality. One person acts,
  the other responds, an adaptive relationship pattern develops, and its
  benefit and cost become visible. Do not assign either person an unsupported
  wound history.
- **Reports:** develop the full argument when the section has room, while
  preserving report register and calculation boundaries.
- **Short cards:** compress to mechanism, recognizable behavior, and
  consequence. Do not imply a life history merely to imitate long-form depth.

Chiron points to the place where experience leaves a mark, even after you have become highly capable at living around it. In Taurus, that mark can form around money, stability, self-worth, and the basic question of what you are allowed to rely on. You may have learned early that having enough was not the same as being able to trust it. Money could disappear. Support could change. Something comfortable could be taken away. So you learned to stretch what you had, lower your expectations, prepare for the loss before it happened, and make yourself feel safe with less.

The difficult part is that survival skills do not automatically retire when the situation changes. You can become so good at protecting yourself from instability that stability itself starts to feel suspicious. You may hesitate to use resources you worked hard to build, stay with the familiar because uncertainty feels more dangerous than dissatisfaction, or keep proving that you can manage without asking whether managing should still be the goal. Chiron in Taurus can make scarcity feel more trustworthy than abundance because scarcity is familiar. You know what to do when there is not enough. The deeper work begins when there is enough and you have to learn how to live differently.

This can also affect the way you measure your own value. Taurus wants something tangible to point to: the money earned, the work completed, the thing you own, the result you can prove. When Chiron is here, it can be difficult to separate what you are worth from what you can produce, preserve, or provide. You may keep working after the point has already been made, hold yourself to a harsher financial standard than you would anyone else, or feel guilty receiving something you did not have to struggle for first. The wound is not simply about wanting more. It is about trusting that you can have something good without immediately preparing for its disappearance.

With Chiron in the 12th house, much of this happens where other people cannot easily see it. You may look completely capable while privately rehearsing every possible loss. You can carry fear for years without naming it because you have already figured out how to function with it. That is one of the strange things about the 12th house: what remains hidden can become so familiar that you stop recognizing how much influence it still has. A decision can look practical on the surface while an old fear is quietly choosing it for you.

Solitude may have become one of the ways you learned to protect yourself. When something hurts, you go inward. You think about it, write about it, make sense of it privately, and usually come back out once you know how to carry it. There is strength in that. But there are experiences you cannot get a new perspective on while you are the only person in the conversation. An old shame can sound completely reasonable when no one else ever hears it.

Healing here is less about becoming fearless than becoming more conscious of which fears are still making decisions for you. You may never become careless with money, trust, or stability, nor should you. The change is subtler than that. You stop treating every good thing as temporary. You stop assuming that needing support makes you vulnerable in the same way it once did. You let someone know when something is hard before you have already solved it by yourself. Over time, the life you built to protect yourself can become a life you are actually able to inhabit.

## Review questions

For applicable long-form work, ask:

1. What is the placement's specific mechanism rather than its topic list?
2. What did the person plausibly learn to do, and why was that response useful?
3. What competence grew from the adaptation?
4. When does that competence keep running after circumstances change?
5. What contradiction makes the costly behavior continue to feel reasonable?
6. Does the house change how the mechanism operates, or merely add keywords?
7. Does the ending change behavior or operating assumptions rather than offer
   generic advice?
8. Could the same argument be moved to another placement? If so, it is not
   astrologically specific enough.

No automated score may establish prose approval. These questions prepare the
passage for the owner's rendered review.
