# Owner writing doctrine (canonical, 2026-08-08) - applies to all TLDR Astro surfaces

Recorded verbatim from the owner. This supersedes and governs every writer prompt, template,
and review standard. The Codex-facing form the owner designated is the final paragraph.

---

The core rule is:
Write the astrology as a recognizable human experience first. Name what is happening, what
someone does because of it, and what it costs or changes. Only then bring in meaning,
perspective, or advice.

Horoscopes: read like something the reader could notice happening in their actual day, week,
or month. Start with the thesis, not the astrology. Then show where it appears: the unanswered
email, the delayed decision, the extra expense, the person asking for more than you have, the
project finally getting attention, the conversation you keep postponing. Give the transit
consequences in work, money, home, body, time, access, recognition, or relationships. Then
give the reader something useful to do with it, arising from the problem just named.
Structure: What is happening -> how it shows up -> why it matters -> what to do with it.
The astrology explains the experience. It should never replace the experience.

Aspects: show what happens when two functions meet. Show the causal chain: Planet A pushes ->
Planet B complicates or supports -> this is what happens in real life. Never keyword-combine.

Synastry: one person does something -> the other responds -> a recognizable pattern develops.
Stay inside the lived relationship dynamic; no coaching lesson tacked on unless the format
calls for it.

Placements: a recurring way someone operates, not an event. The reader learns: what do I tend
to do, notice, need; what happens when the need is frustrated; what does it look like in
ordinary life. No zodiac-character adjective profiles. A known house changes WHERE the pattern
happens, never triggers a textbook paragraph; without birth time the placement must work
houseless.

The voice underneath: concrete without becoming mundane, psychologically accurate without
sounding clinical, warm without becoming therapeutic, astrologically intelligent without
sounding like a textbook, useful without sounding like a life coach. Never make the reader
translate phrases like "issues of control" or "expansive energy" - name the actual issue.
Truth first. Warmth second.

CODEX INSTRUCTION (owner-designated canonical form): Translate every astrological idea into
lived cause and consequence. Begin with the specific human experience, behavior, conflict,
decision, or consequence the astrology describes. Use concrete stakes such as work, money,
home, body, time, access, recognition, and relationships. For aspects, show one force acting
on another. For synastry, show one person doing something and the other reacting. For
placements, describe the recurring behavior and need rather than predicting an event. Add
perspective, warmth, or advice only after the truth has been clearly named. Never make the
reader decode astrology language to understand what is happening.

## Owner-passage-first voice rule (owner-directed, 2026-08-21)

Write from actual Marie Satori passages, not from a description of Marie Satori passages.
Mechanism supplies the astrology. Retrieved owner writing supplies prose behavior. No
assistant-generated wording, synthesized voice summary, or derived style guidance belongs in
the REGISTER evidence pool.

Match what the published prose actually does: where it enters, how quickly it reaches the
point, how much it explains, when it uses a short sentence, when repetition earns emphasis, and
when it stops. Do not impose generic content-design polish, balanced paragraph architecture, or
a mandatory thesis/complication/solution movement. Do not explain an example after the example
has already made the point.

Cold test: could this paragraph sit beside the retrieved owner passages without sounding like
another writer entered the page? If not, it fails the owner gate even when the astrology is
correct.

# Marie voice bank (gold-standard reference)

Companion to WRITING-STANDARD.md. This is the positive reference: Marie's own approved lines, her
this-instead-of-that swaps, and the fuller-voice examples she wants reader-facing copy to match. When a
banned-word rule and this bank disagree, this bank wins, because it is her actual voice.

## How the voice moves (from the examples)

Finish the full thought. Most lines run two or three sentences: a truth, a turn, then a direct instruction
to the reader. Modern, plain, lived. Not clipped fragments, not abstractions. The close is a concrete move
the reader can make, not a tidy summary.

Gold examples (match this shape and level):
1. Sometimes the kindest thing you can do for what you love is loosen your grip. Notice where your constant tending has become interference, and let something grow without you for a while.
2. Nobody becomes a cautionary tale alone. When you see someone lost, look at the isolation that shaped them before you look at the choice they made.
3. You are not required to become a symbol. Your life, your grief, your beauty do not exist to be someone else's content. You are allowed to look the other way.
4. Some people will never run the same inner software you do. Release the fantasy of being fully understood by everyone, and treasure the rare ones who come close.
5. What took you years to build can shift in a moment. Hold your stability with gratitude, not with the assumption that it holds itself.
6. The pain you don't process doesn't disappear. It waits inside you for the next firing. Tend to what's unresolved now, gently, so pressure doesn't find the air pockets later.
7. There is no math that explains why some people leave early. You don't have to solve the equation to carry the love. Let the question soften into remembrance.
8. Your body is a temporary vessel and that's not a tragedy. Aging, letting go, releasing your grip on your prime: this is part of the practice, not the failure of it.
9. No wall is high enough and no hole is deep enough to protect a soul from being alive. Vulnerability isn't the flaw in the design. It is the design.
10. You can clean up every surface and still carry the stain no one sees. Shame doesn't come out in the wash. It comes out in the telling.
11. You don't need their validation, their invitation, or their press. What moves through you cannot be invented by an institution. Create like you already belong, because you do.
12. Notice where money has quietly positioned itself between you and connection. The system wants you sorted into hunter and prey. You get to refuse the roles.
13. The people you admire are holding on just like you are. Release them from the job of saving you. Release yourself from the job of saving anyone. Walk together instead.
14. You carry something wild that no simulated environment can fully tame. When the manufactured world feels too smooth, trust the storm you were born in.
15. Every monument stands on a mayhem someone buried. You're allowed to question a peace that requires an arsenal to maintain it.
16. Your focus is currency. Your attention is sacred. Spend less of it on the tantrums and more of it on the current that actually feeds you.
17. When a giant walks away, the world gets colder and greyer for a while. Grieve the singular voices. Then let their refusal to compromise instruct your own.
18. They want it fast, disposable, and predictable. You can stand in the kitchen with a recipe from scratch anyway. Slowness is not falling behind. It's how the craft survives.
19. Watch how an animal moves through the world without a brand, without metrics, without performance anxiety. That indifference is available to you.
20. You can burn yourself rushing what isn't ready, including you. Every beautiful thing takes a minute to bloom. Surrender to the timing.

## This instead of that (approved swaps)

Format: use the left, never the right.

- "living inside someone else's design" → not "wearing someone else's blueprint"
- "pretending to be normal" → not "performing normalcy"
- "borrowing confidence until it feels like yours" → not "performing confidence"
- "overcompensating with charm" → not "performing charm"
- "going through the motions of authority" → not "performing authority"
- "the radical choice" / "the bold refusal" / "the liberating act" → not "the revolutionary act"
- "not everything that falls apart is a loss. Sometimes it's clearing the way." → not "not everything that falls apart is a failure. Sometimes it's preparation."
- "even moving in flow, needs boundaries" → not "even flow needs banks"
- "self-harm" → not "self-abandonment"
- "Which cycles in my mind am I prepared to interrupt so clarity has space to emerge?" → not "What mental loops am I ready to interrupt so real wisdom can emerge?"

Note on context: "blueprint," "performing," "the room," and "the version of you" are NOT banned words.
Marie uses them ("still your architecture," "the blueprint for something they've never seen," "performing
for the wrong crowd," "make the room rearrange itself," "the version of you that doesn't hold them
accountable"). Only the specific overused constructions on the right are out.

## Write the lived moment (compatibility language)

Do not replace vague language with another psychological phrase. Name the proof, the job, the request, or
the repeated action the reader is actually looking at.

- **"Reassurance"** hides the proof they want. Name it. Not "you need reassurance." Write: "You want them to
  show you that they still care." / "You need to know where you stand." / "You want them to reach back when
  you move closer."
- **"Stay safe"** hides what they're avoiding. Name what they do. Not "they pull back to stay safe." Write:
  "They pull back because they aren't ready to answer." / "They go quieter when they feel pushed to explain
  themselves." / "They need time to understand what they feel before they can share it."
- **"Leaned on"** hides the job they feel they've been handed. Name the job. Not "they feel leaned on."
  Write: "They feel responsible for calming you." / "They feel their answer has to fix everything." / "They
  feel responsible for proving the relationship is still okay."
- **"Say the need out loud"** is generic advice. Name the request. Not "say the need out loud." Write: "Ask
  them to answer instead of going quiet." / "Ask for more affection or time together." / "Tell them whether
  you want comfort, clarity, or a decision."
- **"Steadiness"** names a quality without showing why they trust it. Name what the person repeatedly does.
  Not "their steadiness anchors you." Write: "You trust the way they follow through." / "They don't make
  promises casually." / "They keep showing up, even when their own life is hard."

## Approved one-liner bank (verbatim, by theme)

### Boundaries & energy protection
- If it costs your peace, it's overpriced.
- They'll drain you dry, then blame you for being empty.
- You're allowed to close the door without explaining what's on the other side.
- Don't rent space in your life to people who hate the view.
- You don't have to be available for every crisis.
- If you're the one they call when they're bored in traffic, remember, your time isn't their entertainment. Their commute is their problem to solve.

### Credit, ownership & creative theft
- Stakeholders love a win they never had to work for.
- If they needed your blueprint to build it, it's still your architecture.
- They'll call it 'inspired' to avoid calling it stolen.
- Some people show up to the collab with nothing but open hands.
- If they only call when they need a jumpstart, you're not a partner, you're the power source.
- You weren't brought in to be background lighting.

### Authenticity & self-expression
- Stop dimming your fire to make someone else's candle look brighter.
- They'll love the version of you that's easiest to swallow. Serve 'em the full plate anyway.
- Stop folding yourself into origami just so people can fit you in their pocket.
- You're not here to blend, you're here to make the room rearrange itself.
- Your difference isn't a flaw, it's the blueprint for something they've never seen.
- You weren't built to play background in your own story.
- You can't make waves while keeping your feet dry.
- If they only clap when you're quiet, you're performing for the wrong crowd.
- If your shine makes them blind, they can find shade somewhere else.

### Self-worth & personal power
- You don't have to burn yourself to prove you're fire.
- Sometimes the best thing you can do is put it all down and walk away for the night.
- You're allowed to be off-duty from holding the whole world together.
- You don't have to fix everything today. Or tomorrow.
- Stop building palaces for people who'd leave you homeless.

### Empathy & emotional labor
- You might be feeling tired of being the one who always holds it together. The friend, the organizer, the one who shows up.
- You've carried so much on your own for so long you may not notice when you're pushing past your limits. There's a difference between resilience and never letting yourself rest.
- You're allowed to put the weight down, even if no one else is ready to pick it up.

### Family roles, accountability & breaking patterns
- Some people love the version of you that doesn't hold them accountable.
- Being of the same blood isn't a pass for bad behavior.
- The title of 'parent' or 'sibling' doesn't exempt someone from doing the work.
- You can honor your roots without living tangled in them.
- You can love them and still leave the patterns behind.
- The healthiest thing you can hand down is freedom from generational trauma.

### Family chaos, career & livelihood boundaries
- If keeping the family calm keeps you from keeping a job, it's too expensive.
- You can't pay the bills with the guilt they hand you.
- When family emergencies are constant, they're not emergencies... they're a lifestyle.
- When family emergencies happen so often they feel routine, they're no longer emergencies, they're a lifestyle. Create a support system to create some distance from the chaos.
- Your personal life is not collateral for their chaos.
- You can love them and still answer to your own future first.
- Sometimes the only way to protect your life is to protect your livelihood, because without stability, every other part of your life becomes harder to defend.

### Strategy
- You can have a Plan B without betraying Plan A. The point isn't to split your focus, it's to protect your ability to keep moving if things don't go as planned.
- Success isn't just about winning once. It's about protecting your position so you can try again and again until it sticks.
- Every move should protect two things: the momentum you've built and your capacity to make the next move when the time comes.
- Patience is part of strategy. It isn't about waiting forever. It's the discipline to act when the timing will actually work in your favor. Delayed gratification isn't denial. It's choosing to hold your energy, your resources, and your focus until they can create the most impact.

### Retrograde / review
- Just because you chose it years ago doesn't mean you're still choosing it now. Sometimes you're just moving inside the momentum of a decision you've already outgrown.
- A pause is where you check if the commitment you're honoring is still in service to who you are now.
- You can't see when an old choice has expired if you never stop to check the date.
- Some habits look like discipline from the outside but are just outdated agreements you forgot to renegotiate.
- Review is the grace to admit you've outgrown the old version of yourself who made the choice.
- If you wouldn't choose it today, it might be time to stop living like you did.
- Slowing down isn't falling behind. It's choosing to move with clarity instead of momentum for its own sake.
- Step back far enough to see whether you're fighting for something or just fighting out of habit.
- A pause is not the absence of progress. It's where you gather the truth about where you've been and where you're going.
- Step back far enough to see the pattern before you take another step forward.
- Retrograde seasons are invitations to clean up the mess before you build something new on top of it.
- You can't see the cracks when you're sprinting. Slow down long enough to notice where the light gets in.
- Give people the space to speak. They'll tell you everything you need to know if you're not talking over the reveal.
- Slow down long enough to see if their actions match their words. Most people reveal themselves without realizing it.

### Financial growth & security
- If it doesn't pay you back in the long run, it's an expense, not an investment.
- Growth isn't always about making more. Sometimes it's about keeping more.
- Every dollar you spend tells the story of what you value.

### Self-worth & earning power
- Stop discounting yourself because you think they can't afford you. Let them figure that out.
- You can't build generational wealth on "just happy to be here."
- If you treat yourself like a bargain, don't be surprised when you attract bargain hunters.

### Career & business boundaries
- If they only remember your number when they're in a crisis, you're not on the team. You're on call.
- Know the difference between collaboration and carrying someone.

### Relationships & compromise
- The right person will still piss you off. The difference is you're willing to work through it because the life you're building is worth more than the argument you're having.
- Every couple fights. The ones who last are the ones who know what they're fighting for.

### Health
- You can't outrun stress on caffeine and willpower forever.

### Channeling creativity
- Creativity needs space, not constant productivity.
