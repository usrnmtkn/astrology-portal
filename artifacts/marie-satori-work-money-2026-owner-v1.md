## Money

The money question this year is not only what the work pays. It is what the work costs you to keep doing it.

A higher rate can disappear into a longer commute, more travel, unpaid revisions, extra software, help you now need to hire, or hours you no longer have for anything else. A client can pay well and still be too expensive in time. A better title can come with a schedule you cannot maintain.

Some costs are obvious on a spreadsheet. Others show up because the work now requires the part of the day you needed for appointments, caregiving, recovery, another client, or the project you are trying to build for yourself.

This is especially important in a year with so much private development. You may spend part of 2026 studying, reorganizing, finishing old work, building a new body of work, or leaving a role before its replacement is fully established. Income can move before the larger professional story feels settled. A slower month is a financial fact, not a verdict on the value of the work.

If you run a business, look closely at revenue that depends too heavily on one client, platform, product, or arrangement. More money can increase dependence as easily as it increases freedom. A contract can look profitable until the revisions, support, scope changes, taxes, travel, or unpaid labor are counted.

Use the actual numbers. Look at the rate, hours, commute, expenses, unpaid work, payment timing, revisions, and what another responsibility removes from the rest of the week.

The question is not only how much work is available. It is which work is worth the amount of your life it requires.
