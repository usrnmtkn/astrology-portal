# TLDR Natal Chart Whole-Passage Flow Judge

**Status:** Owner-directed QA standard, 2026-08-12  
**Scope:** Fully rendered natal-chart passages on the You and Friend surfaces  
**Operating mode:** Advisory owner-review evidence only; no automatic rewrite, approval, promotion, or serving change

Review the **entire rendered passage as one piece of prose**.

This is a composition and concept-flow review. It is not an astrology-source audit and it is not a sentence-level grammar check.

Assume the astrology, source routing, and factual inputs have already been checked separately.

Your job is to determine whether the finished passage reads like one human writer developed one clear idea from beginning to end.

## 1. Read it cold

Read only the rendered passage.

Do not use:

* source rows
* drafting notes
* prompt intent
* model reasoning
* corpus explanations
* what you know the writer was trying to say

Judge what the reader actually receives.

If the passage only makes sense because you know the intended interpretation, it fails.

---

## 2. State the core message first

Before judging the prose, write one plain sentence answering:

**What is this passage actually telling the reader?**

The answer should be understandable without astrology jargon.

For example:

> You may keep taking on extra work because you are good at handling it, then realize the job is taking over too much of your schedule.

Or:

> You may think you understood a conversation correctly and act before you realize an important detail was never agreed to.

If you cannot state one clear core message, verdict = **EDIT**.

If the passage appears to contain two or more unrelated core messages that have not been intentionally connected, verdict = **EDIT**.

---

## 3. Check that every paragraph serves the same concept

For each paragraph, ask:

* What new information does this paragraph add?
* Does it deepen the same interpretation?
* Does it introduce a new subject without explaining why?
* Is it repeating something already established?
* Does its example actually demonstrate the core message?
* Does the conclusion follow from what came before?

A passage should develop.

It should not feel like several independently generated astrology statements placed beside one another.

---

## 4. Check sentence-to-sentence logic

Read every sentence in sequence.

After each sentence, ask:

**What would the reader naturally expect to hear next?**

The next sentence should answer or develop that question.

Flag:

* abrupt subject changes
* astrology inserted without a bridge
* examples that appear before the reader knows what they illustrate
* conclusions that introduce new information
* pronouns with unclear referents
* repeated thesis statements
* sentences that contradict an earlier sentence
* changes in certainty that are not explained
* a passage that moves from one life domain to another without showing the connection

The reader should never have to reconstruct the logic.

---

## 5. Check concept continuity

Make sure important words continue to mean the same thing throughout the passage.

For example, do not begin with:

> a problem with your work schedule

then gradually turn it into:

> a conflict about your identity

unless the writing clearly explains how one leads to the other.

Do not move casually between:

* work and career
* friend and coworker
* relationship and collaboration
* money and self-worth
* home and family
* writing and public recognition

These may be astrologically connected, but the prose must show the connection.

The reader should never wonder:

**Are we still talking about the same problem?**

---

## 6. Check example discipline

Examples should clarify the interpretation.

They should not compete with it.

For each concrete example, ask:

* Can I immediately tell what point this example is illustrating?
* Does it belong to the same life domain as the surrounding paragraph?
* Is it adding a useful manifestation or merely another scenario?
* Does the passage contain too many examples for the reader to remember the point?

Prefer a few examples that make the message clearer.

Do not let an example become so detailed that the reader starts following the invented story instead of the astrology.

---

## 7. Check progression

A strong passage usually moves naturally through some version of:

**main message → explanation → recognizable manifestation → consequence**

or:

**situation → underlying pattern → consequence → useful response**

The exact structure can vary.

Do not force visible labels.

But the reader should feel that the passage is going somewhere.

Flag writing that repeatedly resets:

> This can mean...
>
> You may also...
>
> Another possibility...
>
> This transit can also...

That reads like a list, not composed prose.

---

## 8. Check repetition at the idea level

Do not only look for repeated words.

Look for repeated meaning.

These may all be the same sentence in disguise:

> You have too much on your schedule.

> Your week is becoming crowded.

> There are more demands on your time.

> You may not have enough hours for everything.

If one sentence already makes the point, the next sentence should advance it.

Repetition is not depth.

---

## 9. Check the ending

The final sentence or paragraph should feel earned by the passage.

It should:

* complete the idea
* name the consequence
* give a specific useful response when appropriate
* or leave the reader with the central tension clearly understood

It should not suddenly become generic coaching.

Avoid endings such as:

> Trust yourself.

> Find balance.

> Stay grounded.

> Use this energy wisely.

The ending should belong specifically to the passage that came before it.

---

## 10. Read it aloud mentally

Ask:

**Does this sound like one person explaining something clearly to another person?**

Flag passages that sound:

* assembled
* templated
* report-heavy
* over-explained
* overly formal
* mechanically transitional
* like separate source rows stitched together
* like each sentence was written to satisfy a different rule

A passage can contain individually good sentences and still fail this test.

---

# You / Friend voice check

For **You**:

* second person should remain natural and consistent
* avoid repeatedly beginning every sentence with “You may”
* the reader should feel directly addressed, not analyzed

For **Friend**:

* name / they voice should remain consistent
* do not mechanically convert every “you” sentence into `Name + verb`
* the passage should sound like someone explaining the friend's astrology naturally
* avoid repeating the person's name merely to satisfy perspective rules

The You and Friend passages should communicate equivalent astrology without needing identical sentence structure.

---

# Verdicts

Return one verdict:

### PASS

The passage has one clear message, develops naturally, reads smoothly from beginning to end, and contains no meaningful concept or flow problem.

### EDIT

The astrology may be usable, but the passage needs composition work because the message is unclear, repetitive, poorly ordered, awkwardly connected, or split across competing ideas.

### CUT

The passage has no useful distinct message, duplicates another section, or cannot be repaired without replacing most of it.

### SOURCE_GAP

The prose problem is actually caused by insufficient interpretation/source material. Do not invent content to repair it.

---

# Required review output

For each passage return:

**Core message:**
One plain-language sentence.

**Verdict:**
PASS / EDIT / CUT / SOURCE_GAP

**Flow diagnosis:**
Briefly explain where the full passage succeeds or breaks.

**Problem lines:**
Quote only the lines that materially interrupt comprehension or flow.

**Revision instruction:**
Describe what needs to change without rewriting unless a rewrite was explicitly requested.

---

# Hard failure test

A passage cannot receive PASS if any of the following is true:

* the judge cannot summarize the message in one sentence
* the passage changes its central subject without explaining the connection
* examples do not clearly support the central message
* the conclusion does not follow from the body
* repeated sentences restate the same idea instead of developing it
* the reader must know the source astrology to understand why one sentence follows another
* individual sentences are readable but the paragraph as a whole feels assembled rather than written
