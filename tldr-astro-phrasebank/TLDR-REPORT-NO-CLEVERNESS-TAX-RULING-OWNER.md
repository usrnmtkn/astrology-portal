# No-cleverness-tax ruling (owner ruling, canonical, verbatim)

**Status: owner ruling, 2026-08-11, from the first production-report review. REPORT register; refines the lived-prose standard and the compression evidence in the calibration corpus. Supersedes any corpus signal that rewards compression requiring reader decoding — including previously praised lines now explicitly rejected below. The four checks join the report critique checklist; the rejected/replacement pairs are labeled corpus evidence (provenance: owner-rejected / owner-authored replacement). Second person REMAINS the report voice; the "pronoun leak" recommendation from the external critique is REJECTED by the owner. Rhetorical micro-quotes ("sure, I'll do it") remain allowed, per the owner's replacement sentences.**

## The rule (owner text verbatim)

```text
New report rule: no cleverness tax

The writer must do the interpretation before the sentence reaches the reader.
If a sentence depends on the reader translating a metaphor, compressed phrase,
or abstract noun into the actual life situation, rewrite it.

The issue is not that these sentences are technically unclear. It is that they
are compressed in a way that sounds written. The reader has to stop and unpack
them. For this report, the writing should feel easier than that.
```

## Owner-rejected constructions (labeled negative evidence)

```text
"Summer opens the year outward."
"The work starts moving between people."
"Circumstances may choose part of it for you."
"August brings greater access and response."
"Wednesday still has one afternoon."
"The public and private parts of life compete."
"The opportunity reaches the calendar."
```

And replace them with what is actually happening:

```text
You start sharing the work.
Someone replies.
A meeting gets added.
The deadline requires revisions.
Your schedule changes because caregiving changed.
You want the opportunity, but it requires hours you were already using elsewhere.
You say yes to something good and later realize the preparation, travel, and
follow-up took most of the day.
```

## Owner before/after pairs (labeled corpus evidence)

**REJECTED:** "Circumstances may choose part of it for you. Either way, being capable of continuing is not the same as wanting to keep organizing your life around the same terms."
**OWNER REPLACEMENT:** "The change may be your decision, or it may happen because your health, schedule, caregiving responsibilities, finances, location, or access have changed. You can still be capable of doing something and no longer want your life organized around it."

**REJECTED:** "Summer opens the year outward."
**OWNER REPLACEMENT:** "By summer, work you have been developing privately starts reaching other people." — then made concrete: "You may start showing the work to people you trust, sending it to someone who can respond, talking about it more openly, or putting it into a form that can be submitted or published." (Note: trusted-friend feedback is one good branch of the manifestation set, not the promised outcome.)

**REJECTED:** "August brings greater access and response, but it also shows exactly how quickly a worthwhile opportunity can crowd the week." (Report language: access, response, worthwhile opportunity, crowd the week are abstractions piled together.)
**OWNER REPLACEMENT:** "By August, more people may be reading, replying to, or asking about what you have been working on. That can bring useful opportunities, but every meeting, revision, invitation, or deadline also has to fit into the same week you already had."

**REJECTED (including the previously praised line — prior editorial judgment reversed by owner):** "The work may matter. The invitation may be worth accepting. The conversation may genuinely need to happen. The problem starts when lunch moves again, sleep gets shorter, an appointment is pushed into next week, and one easy yes quietly takes three hours you did not have. The opportunity may be worthwhile. Wednesday still has one afternoon."
**OWNER REPLACEMENT:** "Overcommitting doesn't always happen because of bad choices. It usually happens when you say yes to good things. But you still only have so many hours in a day, so a quick 'sure, I'll do it' ends up cutting into your lunch, your sleep, and the plans you already made."

**Why the replacement works (owner analysis, verbatim):**

```text
* It names the mechanism first: overcommitment can come from good choices.
* It explains why: finite hours.
* It shows the consequence in ordinary life: lunch, sleep, existing plans.
* Nothing needs to be decoded.
* It sounds like someone explaining something useful to you, not writing a
  report about you.
```

## Second person: abstract vs recognizable (owner ruling, verbatim)

```text
Second person is not the problem. The problem is abstract second person versus
recognizable second person.

"You may experience increased visibility and changing obligations." is weak.

"You may get the opportunity you wanted and realize it requires two meetings,
a trip, and three days of follow-up." is strong.
```

## The four checks (join the report critique checklist)

```text
1. Can the reader understand every sentence on the first read?
2. Did I name what actually happens instead of summarizing it with an abstract
   phrase?
3. Did I explain the cause before trying to make the sentence memorable?
4. If I removed the astrology, would this still sound like a useful description
   of a recognizable life situation?
```
