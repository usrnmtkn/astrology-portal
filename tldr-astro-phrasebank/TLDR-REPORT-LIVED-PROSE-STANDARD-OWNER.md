# Lived-prose generation standard (owner ruling, canonical, verbatim)

**Status: owner ruling, 2026-08-09. Generation rule, not reader-facing copy. Applies to ALL report domains (general, work_money, love_connection, personal_health) and all horizons — this is a prose-layer standard, additive to the 27-point GENERATION STANDARD in `TLDR-YEAR-AHEAD-GENERATION-LOGIC-OWNER.md` and to each domain's canonical prompt. Source: the owner's surgical final pass on the Personal & Health report (reference implementation: `artifacts/marie-satori-personal-health-2026-owner-v1.md`, owner-authored final). Owner framing: "The biggest lesson from this report is that accurate manifestations are not enough. The generator needs a second reasoning layer between interpretation and finished prose."**

**The final flatness check (section: "Final flatness check") is also discrimination evidence for the critique checklist and judge rubric: its ten questions are the owner's operational definition of lived vs. flat prose, and the example passages here are owner-labeled positive/negative pairs.**

Owner text verbatim below. **Owner amendment, 2026-08-09:** flatness question 9 reworded by the owner during the v3 judge review to prevent the critic from treating broadly relatable ordinary-language sentences as automatically off-voice; the diagnostic is corroborative, not standalone.

```text
Generator logic: how to get this writing instead of flat report prose

The biggest lesson from this report is that accurate manifestations are not enough. The generator needs a second reasoning layer between interpretation and finished prose.

The generation path should be:
astrology → domain → lived circumstance → consequence → complication → prose
not:
astrology → concept → explanation → advice

1. Keep the writer inside the reader's life

The writer should never describe what "the report" is doing.
Avoid constructions such as:

* Health enters this report...
* The Personal & Health story is...
* This transit gives you information...
* The second pass gives you...
* What matters here is...
* This section is about...
* The question becomes...
* The point is...
* This distinction matters...

These are writer notes that leaked into reader-facing copy.
Instead, state what happens:
Late August is where your health routines are most likely to show how crowded the week has become.
Not:
Health enters this report most clearly in late August.
The reader does not need to know why the writer included the paragraph.

2. Do not explain an observation after the observation already worked

If the prose says:
A text arrives and your afternoon is gone. Someone asks for a small favor, and the evening you kept clear for yourself disappears before you even realize you said yes.
Do not follow with:
This year can make that pattern much more obvious.
The example already made the pattern obvious.
Move forward.
A strong manifestation should not need a sentence explaining that it was a manifestation.

3. Replace abstract consequence words with the actual consequence

Words such as these are not automatically banned:

* capacity
* cost
* room
* sustainability
* energy
* access
* balance
* support

But they should not carry the sentence when something more observable exists.
Flat:
The activity may cost more energy than it used to.
Better reasoning:
What changes afterward?
Possible answer:
The errands still need to get done. The appointment is still at ten. Work still starts in the morning.
Flat:
The next day has less room.
Ask:
Less room for what?
Then name it.
Flat:
The conditions around the activity may need to change.
Ask:
Which conditions?
Then write:
You may still take the trip and leave the next morning open. You may keep the job and change the hours.

4. Concrete nouns must participate in cause and consequence

A sentence does not become lived merely because it contains concrete nouns.
Flat:
Work, appointments, travel, caregiving, and recovery may affect your capacity.
The nouns are concrete. The writing is not.
The generator must show how one changes another:
Caregiving takes the afternoon that used to absorb errands.
Work gets busy and the appointment moves again.
Travel still works, but not if you come home late Sunday and start again at eight Monday morning.
The test is not "are there concrete nouns?"
The test is:
Can the reader see what changed because of what?

5. Look for the human contradiction before drafting

Many of the strongest passages in this report contain two truths at the same time.
Examples:
You can still be good at something and feel completely finished with how much of your life it takes to keep doing it.
A long day may still be manageable. The problem can be what is waiting for you afterward.
You may want most of what is filling the week.
The opportunity may still be worth taking, but the version that fits may not look exactly like the one first offered.
Before writing a major paragraph, ask:
What two seemingly conflicting facts are both true here?
Examples:

* I still want this, and the old conditions no longer work.
* I can still do this, and I cannot keep arranging the week around it the same way.
* These are good opportunities, and there are too many of them for one Wednesday.
* The new routine is supposed to simplify life, and maintaining it has become another responsibility.
* The role still works from the outside because the reader keeps absorbing what makes it unworkable.

That contradiction creates the point of view.

6. Give abstract astrology an ordinary place to land

For every major factor, ask:
Where does this show up on Tuesday?
Not literally Tuesday every time. The instruction means:
Where does the astrology become observable?
Possible locations:

* the calendar
* the commute
* an appointment
* the grocery run
* a text thread
* the morning after travel
* the empty afternoon
* the meeting that gets added
* the repair
* the staircase
* the drive
* the lunch that gets delayed
* the deadline
* the family responsibility

Example:
Saturn is not merely "structure."
Saturn becomes:
"I need more notice" becomes three days instead of three hours.
That is astrology translated into life.

7. Let astrology explain the life, not interrupt it

A strong paragraph often moves:
recognizable situation → astrology → consequence
or:
astrology → immediately translated circumstance → complication
Example:
Jupiter squares your sixth-house Moon on August 27, and the first signs may be very ordinary: lunch keeps getting pushed later, an appointment moves again, sleep gets shorter, or a routine you usually keep without thinking suddenly needs planning.
Do not create a separate textbook paragraph explaining the Moon, Jupiter, and sixth house before giving manifestations.
The interpretation should remain visible, but astrology should be doing work inside the paragraph.

8. Multi-pass transits must advance the story

Do not explain the same transit three times.
For Saturn sextile Ascendant:
First pass, May: make the change specific.
"I need more notice" becomes three days instead of three hours.
Second pass, October: find out what survived.
Working from home solved the commute but made it harder to stop working.
Final pass, February: keep the version that has already proved useful.
Each pass has a different narrative job.
Do not regenerate the original manifestation menu.

9. Advice should emerge from the circumstance

Avoid generic advice such as:

* listen to your body
* protect your energy
* honor your needs
* prioritize self-care
* create a sustainable routine
* trust the evidence
* make more room for yourself

Instead, identify the actual change.
The appointment cannot be the flexible part forever.
You may still take the trip and leave the next morning open.
"I need more notice" becomes three days instead of three hours.
The recommendation should be the next practical move inside the situation, not a lesson extracted from it.

10. Do not manufacture a polished closer

One of the biggest sources of voice drift was forcing every paragraph to finish with wisdom.
Avoid:
This allows you to create a life that supports who you are becoming.
The goal is to build a life that leaves room for you.
Trust what your body is telling you.
This distinction can preserve more of what matters.
If the observation already landed, stop.
Sometimes the correct closer is:
The appointment is still at ten. Work still starts in the morning.
Sometimes it is:
You know because you have lived both versions.
Sometimes the paragraph simply moves into the next development.

11. Preserve natural paragraph rhythm

Do not create artificial drama by putting every short sentence on a separate line.
Use short sentences inside real paragraphs:
A long day may still be manageable. The problem can be what is waiting for you afterward. The errands still need to get done. The appointment is still at ten. Work still starts in the morning.
The rhythm comes from sentence length and sequence, not formatting tricks.

12. Do not over-polish the lived details

Ordinary language is often stronger.
Prefer:
A text arrives and your afternoon is gone.
Not:
An incoming message unexpectedly reorganizes your available time.
Prefer:
The appointment is still at ten.
Not:
Existing obligations remain fixed despite reduced capacity.
Prefer:
Wednesday still has one afternoon.
Not:
The available time remains finite despite increased opportunity.
If the plain sentence already carries the meaning, leave it alone.

Regeneration procedure for the AI writer

For every paragraph that feels flat, do not immediately rewrite it.
First extract:
ASTROLOGY
What factor is actually being interpreted?
LIVED FACT
What can happen in ordinary life?
CAUSE
What changed?
CONSEQUENCE
What now gets moved, delayed, added, cancelled, divided, rescheduled, or made harder?
CONTRADICTION
What two things can both be true?
DO NOT ASSUME
What outcome, diagnosis, status, or motivation must remain open?
Then draft.
Example:
ASTROLOGY: Jupiter square sixth-house Moon
LIVED FACT: schedule expands
CAUSE: more plans, communication, errands, responsibilities
CONSEQUENCE: lunch moves, appointment moves, sleep shortens
CONTRADICTION: reader may genuinely want the new opportunities
DO NOT ASSUME: illness or medical crisis
Then write:
The difficult part is that you may want most of what is filling the week. The class sounds interesting. The trip is worth taking. The project matters. You want to see the person who invited you. Jupiter makes one more yes feel easy while you are saying it. The problem shows up later when Wednesday still has one afternoon and four different plans expecting to fit inside it.
That is the model.

Final flatness check

Before accepting a unit, ask:

1. Does the paragraph contain something a person could actually recognize happening?
2. Can I tell what caused what?
3. Did I name the actual consequence instead of "cost," "capacity," "room," or another abstraction?
4. Is there a human contradiction or meaningful observation, not just information?
5. Does the astrology explain the circumstance instead of sitting beside it?
6. Did any writer-facing language leak into the reader-facing report?
7. Did I explain an example that had already made the point?
8. Did I force an inspirational closer after the paragraph was already finished?
9. Could the interpretive passage move unchanged into a generic wellness, HR, or horoscope article because it lacks the unit-specific circumstance, cause, or consequence? If so, identify what is missing. This diagnostic alone cannot establish a defect.
10. Would removing the final sentence make the paragraph stronger? If yes, remove it.

The final target is not merely "specific." It is observed. The reader should recognize their Wednesday, their ten o'clock appointment, their commute, their text message, or the responsibility that somehow became five responsibilities before the prose tells them what any of it means.
```

## Owner amendment, 2026-08-09 (second ruling): astrology sentence → lived consequence

**Status: owner ruling, engine-level, verbatim. Issued during review of two approved knowledge-matrix rows; the owner designated those two approved lines as calibration exemplars for this rule. Applies to ALL writing surfaces that consume this standard: report domains, knowledge-matrix voiced rows, sky placements, and horoscope copy. Additive to the standard above; where they overlap, this amendment sharpens the enforcement, it does not soften it.**

Owner text verbatim:

```text
The two approved examples establish an important rule: once the astrology has been named, the prose should move forward into lived experience instead of translating the astrology again.

The engine tuning I would carry forward is:

* Astrology sentence → lived consequence. After the placement/aspect is established, the next sentence should usually show what somebody notices, does, avoids, changes, says, feels in their body, or has to deal with.
* Do not explain the same mechanism twice. A sentence like "Saturn square Uranus creates tension between responsibility and freedom" should not be followed by another abstract sentence explaining responsibility versus freedom. Move to what happens because of it.
* Watch interpretation-engine scaffolds. Especially repeated constructions such as "directs emotional pressure into", "the opportunity is", "the adjustment is", "the person has more room to", "the tension eases when", "this creates an opening for", and similar explanatory bridges. They can appear occasionally, but they should not become the default grammar.
* Advice should emerge from the observation.
Better: "By the time freedom starts looking like an emergency exit, the problem has usually been building for a while."
Less effective: "The tension eases when responsibilities are handled deliberately."
* Concrete means observable consequence, not decorative detail.
"A problem gets fixed. The work gets organized." is concrete because it shows what 6th-house energy actually does. It does not need a random object or invented scene.
* Let the contradiction carry the warmth.
"The trouble starts when staying busy becomes a way to avoid the feeling." sounds human because it understands why the behavior works before showing where it stops working.
* Do not automatically resolve the paragraph. Sometimes the sharpest ending is the consequence, contradiction, or realization rather than a coaching instruction.
* Every sentence after the astrology has to earn new information. If it merely paraphrases the prior sentence in softer language, cut it or replace it with lived evidence.

And I would add one explicit engine test:
After the astrology sentence, ask: What becomes observable because this is true?
If the next sentence answers the astrology again instead of answering that question, the engine should flag it for revision.
```

**Owner clarification, 2026-08-09 (same day):** the announcement construction "planet enters sign, attention turns to A, B, and C" is acceptable phrasing; variation is good. It is NOT a defect on its own. The defect arises only when (a) it is followed by a sentence that re-translates the astrology instead of showing a lived consequence, or (b) it becomes the only grammar across a batch. Judges must not flag this family as keyword-stack or scaffold merely for existing.

**Owner amendment, 2026-08-10 (third ruling): the word "real" as generator filler.** Owner text verbatim:

```text
I want a rule against the over-use of the word "real". Real becomes a lazy ai-generator fill. Instead take time to construct a thoughtful sentence.
"The opportunity becomes real when you are willing to learn." - "The opportunity becomes a lesson, when you are open to the experience to learn."
```

Enforcement (`real-filler` defect): constructions where "real" carries the transformation or intensity that a concrete noun should carry — "becomes real," "gets real," "feels real," "make it real," "the real work," "real progress" — are the primary targets. The fix is never a synonym swap; it is naming what the thing actually becomes (a lesson, a bill, a schedule, a conversation, a decision). "Real" used literally and sparingly (a real request, a real week) is acceptable; recurrence as a crutch is the defect. This extends the phrasebook's existing ban on "makes this feel real." Frequency rule: more than one "real" per unit, or any "becomes/gets/feels real" construction, triggers the flag.

**Owner amendment, 2026-08-10 (fourth ruling): the reader should never have to translate the writing.** Owner text verbatim:

```text
The paragraph is trying to sound written instead of simply telling the reader what happens. "Fire," "target," "cleanup," and "charging toward" all force the reader to translate the sentence back into ordinary behavior.

The rule should be stricter than the V12 audit was: the reader should never have to translate the writing. A concrete example is useful. A metaphor is only useful when it makes the meaning more obvious, not more literary. So phrases such as "the fire is not the problem," "find the shelf empty," "emotional weather," "holding the key," "the door opens," "the tide changes," or "give it a target" should all be treated as audit flags unless the sentence would be less clear without them.
```

Owner-approved calibration exemplar (Mars in Aries, exact wording):

```text
Mars in Aries tends to act quickly. You make the call, take the lead, start the workout, speak up, or deal with a problem as soon as you notice it. Waiting can be frustrating, especially when you already know what you want to do.

That speed can help you take initiative, but it can also lead you to argue, take a risk, or make a decision before you have enough information. Not every situation needs an immediate response. Give yourself enough time to understand what is happening before you act.
```

Enforcement (`translation-required` defect): any figurative phrase the reader must decode back into behavior is a flag. The test is not "is it a metaphor" but "would the sentence be less clear without it." Rewrites replace the image with the ordinary behavior it stands for (make the call, argue, take a risk, commit to a plan). This is stricter than, and takes precedence over, earlier tolerance for grounded metaphor on natal-delineation and knowledge-matrix surfaces; the phrasebook's metaphor families remain available only where they pass the clarity test.

### Enforcement notes (implementation, not owner prose)

- **Critique checklist**: add the observability test as a named defect class, `astrology-restated`. The critique flags any sentence directly following an astrology-naming sentence that re-translates the mechanism instead of answering "what becomes observable because this is true?" The two owner-approved matrix rows serve as positive calibration; the "Saturn square Uranus" double-explanation serves as the negative exemplar.
- **Scaffold constructions** ("directs emotional pressure into", "the opportunity is", "the adjustment is", "the person has more room to", "the tension eases when", "this creates an opening for"): warn-level, frequency-based, not a hard ban. One instance in a unit is acceptable; recurrence as default grammar is the defect (`scaffold-grammar`). Deterministic lint may count occurrences per unit and per batch; the judge decides whether density constitutes the defect.
- **Closer rule**: extends standard point 10 — the reviser may not append a coaching instruction to a paragraph whose consequence, contradiction, or realization already ends it.
- **New-information rule**: extends flatness question 7 — a sentence that paraphrases its predecessor in softer language is a defect (`soft-paraphrase`) even when it contains no banned vocabulary.
