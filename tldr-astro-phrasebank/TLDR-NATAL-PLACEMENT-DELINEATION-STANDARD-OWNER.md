# Natal placement delineation standard (owner ruling, canonical, verbatim)

**Status: owner ruling, 2026-08-10. Generation and judge rule, not reader-facing copy. Applies to natal delineation surfaces: sign-on-house (including empty houses), ruler placements, and natal placement writing generally — the Ruiz empty-houses matrix review and all remaining delineations use this as the model. Companion to the lived-prose standard (`TLDR-REPORT-LIVED-PROSE-STANDARD-OWNER.md`) and its 2026-08-09 "astrology sentence → lived consequence" amendment; this ruling defines the same move for natal writing, where there is no transit chronology to carry the story.**

## The benchmark (owner-designated)

```text
Capricorn on an empty 10th house can make you the person people trust with consequential responsibilities. You get handed the keys, the budget, the difficult client, or the deadline nobody wants to miss because people expect you to handle what matters. Over time, that competence can become part of your reputation, and recognition may arrive because you have already proven what you can carry. The harder part is noticing when being dependable has turned into being responsible for more than is actually yours.
```

## Owner text verbatim

```text
What makes it work is that the astrology is translated into a specific human role immediately, instead of starting with a trait:

Capricorn on an empty 10th house can make you the person people trust with consequential responsibilities.

That is stronger than "responsibility becomes part of your reputation" because the reader immediately understands what happens between them and other people.

Then the examples are not random 10th-house nouns. They all prove the same mechanism:

You get handed the keys, the budget, the difficult client, or the deadline nobody wants to miss because people expect you to handle what matters.

The important piece is because. The keys and budget are not decoration. They show cause and consequence.

Then it follows the pattern across time:

Over time, that competence can become part of your reputation, and recognition may arrive because you have already proven what you can carry.

And only after the strength has been fully established does the complication arrive:

The harder part is noticing when being dependable has turned into being responsible for more than is actually yours.

So the model is essentially:
Astrological mechanism → human role → recognizable evidence → consequence over time → natural complication.

Not:
Sign trait → house keywords → list of examples → generic warning.

And the tone matters just as much. This example is direct, adult, specific, and generous without flattering the reader. It doesn't say Capricorn is ambitious, disciplined, responsible, hardworking, or authoritative. It lets the reader infer all of that from being the person who gets handed the keys, budget, difficult client, and important deadline.

That is the writing level I would use as the model for the remaining delineations.
```

## Judge rubric (implementation, not owner prose)

The five beats, in order. A delineation may compress beats but may not reorder the complication before the strength or replace the role with a trait list.

1. **Mechanism → role** (`trait-first` defect): the opening sentence must place the reader in a specific human role among other people. Opening with a trait adjective ("Capricorn here makes you disciplined and ambitious") or an abstract restatement ("responsibility becomes part of your reputation") is the defect.
2. **Evidence proves the mechanism** (`decorative-evidence` defect): examples must share one mechanism and carry a "because" — cause and consequence visible in the sentence. House nouns listed without a connecting cause ("career, reputation, ambition, and public life are highlighted") are decoration, not evidence.
3. **Consequence over time**: the pattern compounds ("over time... because you have already proven..."). Optional in short rows, but when present it must follow from the evidence, not introduce a new claim.
4. **Complication arrives after the strength** (`premature-complication` defect): the natural cost appears only once the strength is established, and it grows out of the same mechanism ("dependable has turned into responsible for more than is actually yours"). A generic warning bolted on ("be careful not to overwork") is the defect; so is opening with the problem.
5. **Tone** (`trait-naming` defect): direct, adult, specific, generous without flattery. Never name the trait the evidence implies. If the row says "ambitious, disciplined, responsible, hardworking, or authoritative," the evidence has been replaced by its summary.

Shared invariants still apply: tentative verbs where the claim is experiential, no em dashes, no "whether" false-choices, no banned vocabulary, no manufactured closers, and the 2026-08-09 clarification that approved constructions are not defects on their own — variation is good.
