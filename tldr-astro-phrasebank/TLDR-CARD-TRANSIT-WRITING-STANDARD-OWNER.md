# Card-register transit writing standard (owner ruling, canonical, verbatim)

**Status: owner ruling, 2026-08-09. Generation rule, not reader-facing copy. Canonical for the CARD register: all planetary transit-to-natal files and short app surfaces. Derived from the Jupiter transit-to-natal rewrite process (reference implementation: `TLDR-JUPITER-TRANSIT-NATAL-FAMILY-OWNER.md`, owner-approved). Companions: `TLDR-REGISTER-PER-SURFACE-RULING-OWNER.md` (surface scoping), `TLDR-REPORT-LIVED-PROSE-STANDARD-OWNER.md` (the report-register counterpart). Section 23 is the top-level Codex direction for rewriting the remaining planetary files. Sections 20–21 feed the critique checklist example library and the card-harness rubric directly. Owner text verbatim below.**

```text
The Jupiter rewrite exposed a much clearer set of rules for getting the rest of the files to this level. The important change was not simply "write more like CC." It was where the writing begins, how the astrology enters, and what counts as a finished observation.

Canonical writing rules for bringing existing files to this level

1. The writing movement

The default movement should be:
lived observation → recognizable circumstance → consequence → astrology explains why → useful direction
Not:
aspect definition → astrology keywords → possible manifestations → advice
The reader should understand the experience before they are asked to understand the astrology.
Wrong
Jupiter expands the public role, creating opportunities for increased visibility and recognition.
Technically correct. Dead on arrival.
Right
More people are paying attention now.
Then:
Something you have been building may be ready for a larger audience. Your name gets passed around. A project gets published. A client wants more.
Then astrology:
Jupiter is crossing your Midheaven, expanding the part of your life other people can see and recognize.
The astrology explains the observation. It does not substitute for the observation.

2. Observation comes before abstraction

Every entry needs a sentence that a person could recognize happening in their life.
Good:

* The opportunity is getting bigger. The agreement around it is not.
* Work is asking for more. Home may be doing the same.
* The idea is persuasive. Check the details anyway.
* One more yes can take the rest of the day with it.
* A beautiful story can still need receipts.
* Your plan may run straight into somebody else's.
* The bigger plan needs a real structure.
* Freedom gets loud.
* Generosity can get expensive fast.

These work because they already contain tension.
Weak:

* Growth creates friction with the current structure.
* Expansion may increase public visibility.
* Opportunity becomes available.
* Emotional needs may be amplified.
* A supportive aspect creates ease.

Those are notes for the astrologer, not prose for the reader.

3. Do not make the aspect families artificially symmetrical

This was one of the major causes of drift.
The writing got worse when every aspect was forced into polished matching definitions:
Conjunction = expansion
Sextile = opportunity
Square = tension
Trine = ease
Opposition = polarity
That logic belongs underneath the writing, but the sentences should not sound manufactured from the same mold.
For Jupiter, the useful internal logic became:
Conjunction
The natal function itself gets bigger.
Ask:
What happens when there is suddenly more of this?
Sextile
There is a usable opening.
Ask:
What ordinary invitation, introduction, response, resource, or opportunity gives the reader somewhere to go next?
Participation matters.
Square
The increase does not fit inside the existing arrangement.
Ask:
What was built for a smaller version of this?
This is why:
The opportunity is getting bigger. The agreement around it is not.
worked so much better than:
Growth no longer fits neatly inside the current public setup.
Trine
Something already moving receives help.
Ask:
What becomes easier to receive, accomplish, communicate, or support?
Then look for the Jupiter complication:
What happens when several easy yeses arrive together?
Opposition
The increase shows up across from the reader.
Ask:
Who or what on the other side now wants more too?
This can be another person, home versus work, duty versus opportunity, one plan versus another, or another side of the axis.

4. Jupiter's job must stay Jupiter's job

The approved logic was:
Jupiter's first job is increase, not good news.
Before writing, answer:
What becomes more?
It may be:

* attention
* responsibility
* requests
* possibility
* appetite
* confidence
* money being spent
* people involved
* information
* desire
* emotional investment
* workload
* leverage
* options
* invitations
* uncertainty
* faith
* pressure to grow

Then ask:
What does having more of that actually change?
That second question creates the writing.
Example:
Jupiter + Mars is not simply:
more energy.
It becomes:
You have more fuel than patience.
Then:
A difficult task feels possible. A project gains speed. You may also turn every obstacle into a dare.
That is Jupiter doing Jupiter's job through Mars.

5. Planet logic must produce behavior

Do not write the planet as a keyword.
Translate it.
Mercury
Not:
communication, information, thinking
Ask:

* What gets said?
* What gets misunderstood?
* What needs confirmation?
* What promise gets larger than the facts?
* What email actually needs sending?
* What detail changes the agreement?

Thus:
The idea is persuasive. Check the details anyway.
Moon
Not:
emotions, care, home
Ask:

* Who needs something?
* What gets moved because of it?
* What did the reader agree to?
* Does one afternoon become a recurring responsibility?
* What happens to meals, sleep, appointments, home?

Thus:
One more yes can take the rest of the day with it.
Saturn
Not:
limits and responsibility
Ask:

* What is fixed?
* What takes longer?
* What cannot fit under the old terms?
* What needs a schedule?
* What remains true after enthusiasm passes?

Thus:
The opportunity arrives with a limit attached.
Uranus
Not:
freedom, rebellion, disruption
Ask:

* What changed?
* What stopped fitting?
* What new option became possible?
* Was the change chosen or did circumstances change first?

Thus:
A new option can change the whole map.
Neptune
Not:
dreams, spirituality, confusion
Ask:

* What sounds convincing?
* What is still undefined?
* What is hoped for versus confirmed?
* What idea has emotional or spiritual meaning before it has practical form?

Thus:
A beautiful story can still need receipts.
Pluto
Not:
transformation and power
Ask:

* Who controls access?
* Who can change the terms?
* Who has the signature?
* What becomes consequential?
* What does increased leverage allow someone to do?

Thus:
More power does not make the terms fair.
Venus
Not:
love, pleasure, beauty
Ask:

* What gets bought?
* Who gets invited?
* What feels good enough to say yes to?
* How does affection turn into time, money, access, or effort?

Thus:
Generosity can get expensive fast.

6. Concrete nouns need to do something

One of the recurring problems was keyword stacking.
Bad:
Work, money, clients, recognition, publishing, leadership, titles, reputation, and public opportunities may expand.
That is technically specific and experientially vague.
Better:
A proposal gets accepted. Someone recommends you before you ask. Credit comes without another round of reminding everyone whose idea it was.
Concrete nouns and events should participate in cause and consequence.
The email should arrive.
The deadline should move.
The commute should change.
The client should ask for more.
The appointment should still be at ten.
The title should fail to match the job.
The bill should exist.
The extra meeting should take the afternoon.

7. Use ordinary consequences instead of conceptual consequences

Bad:
This may challenge your capacity.
Ask what that means.
Maybe:
The late meeting takes the only evening you had free.
Bad:
There may be tension between private and public demands.
Maybe:
A move changes the commute just as work starts asking for more hours.
Bad:
Your responsibilities may expand.
Maybe:
Someone gives you more responsibility without explaining what comes off your plate.
Bad:
Be mindful of overcommitment.
Maybe:
Three easy yeses can create one impossible week.
That last sentence is especially representative of the level we reached.

8. Advice has to be earned by the scene

Do not bolt generic coaching onto the end.
Bad:
Remember to maintain balance and protect your energy.
Bad:
Stay grounded and trust yourself.
Bad:
Be mindful of overextension.
Instead, the circumstance should tell us what action makes sense.
If the problem is:
You may be doing a bigger job before the title, pay, authority, or support has caught up.
Then the direction can be:
More work may deserve more money. More responsibility may need more authority. A bigger project may need another deadline.
That advice belongs specifically to this problem.

9. Warmth comes after the truth

The CC screenshots helped clarify this.
What we wanted from that style was:

* confidence
* warmth
* cadence
* a little wit
* permission
* direct address
* point of view

What we did not want to import:

* generic wellness language
* inner-child language by default
* therapeutic abstractions
* "healing journey" framing
* vague self-care instructions
* social-justice/nonprofit framing that is not native to the astrology
* inspirational conclusions that float above the actual situation

So:
Take the win.
works.
Follow the introduction.
works.
Let yourself enjoy what is working.
can work because the preceding scene earned it.
But:
Honor your needs and give yourself permission to grow.
is generic enough to go anywhere.

10. A little personality is necessary

A major user correction in this process was essentially:
These phrases have drifted. This is more of the style.
The earlier rewrites were becoming technically impeccable and stylistically lifeless.
The stronger level allows sentences such as:
A beautiful story can still need receipts.
Freedom gets loud.
The words are working for you.
The energy is real. The brakes need to be real too.
The opportunity is getting bigger. The agreement around it is not.
Follow the introduction.
These are not decoration. They create a point of view before the explanatory material begins.

11. Do not explain a sentence after it already worked

This is crucial.
If you write:
Three easy yeses can create one impossible week.
Do not follow it with:
This means that although opportunities may feel positive individually, collectively they can create scheduling challenges.
The second sentence kills the first.
Trust the observation.
Likewise:
A career can change before the résumé does.
does not need:
In other words, internal professional changes can occur before they become externally visible.
Stop after the good sentence.

12. Do not end every entry with polished wisdom

Generated writing often tries to conclude.
It produces:
Ultimately, growth is about learning how to expand without losing yourself.
That is exactly the kind of line to cut.
A better ending may simply be:
The arrangement may be what needs to change.
Or:
At some point, the problem may not be your effort.
Or:
Three easy yeses can create one impossible week.
The ending should feel like the consequence became clear, not like the writer climbed onto a stage.

13. Preserve contradiction

Your stronger writing often allows two facts to remain true.
Examples:
You may still want the work and no longer be able to do it under the old conditions.
Someone can be very interested in you and still be a poor fit.
You may genuinely want both and still find that the same hours cannot belong to both.
The opportunity may be real. The current role may matter too.
This is much stronger than making every transit a simple positive or negative verdict.
Human contradiction is one of the main sources of emotional precision in the voice.

14. Do not confuse specificity with lists

This was a major correction during the annual reports too.
Bad:
Travel, relocation, legal matters, publishing, education, applications, paperwork, and long-range plans may change.
That is a list of domains.
Better:
Something important depends on an approval, and the person making the final decision is not the person you have been talking to.
That is a circumstance.
Specificity means cause + consequence, not more nouns.

15. The reader-facing headline should contain the thesis

A strong headline should already tell the reader what the transit feels like.
Examples from the Jupiter pass:

* More people are paying attention now.
* Follow the introduction.
* The opportunity is getting bigger. The agreement around it is not.
* Work is asking for more. Home may be doing the same.
* The idea gets bigger, and so does the conversation.
* One more yes can take the rest of the day with it.
* The dream gets convincing fast.
* The stakes are getting higher.
* The opportunity arrives with a limit attached.
* Put your name on it.
* Freedom gets loud.
* Generosity can get expensive fast.

Avoid headings like:

* Expanding Visibility
* Growth Opportunity
* Public Recognition
* Emotional Expansion
* Productive Tension
* Beneficial Change

Those belong in taxonomy, not reader copy.

16. The technical astrology should sit underneath the prose

A useful structure for these transit entries is:
Headline
A human thesis.
Paragraph 1
What could actually happen.
Paragraph 2
Why this astrology creates that situation, plus the useful response.
Attribution
The astro: Jupiter square your Midheaven
The astrology is still present. It simply does not force the reader to decode it before anything meaningful happens.

17. Keep `Do not assume` internal

These guardrails were useful in the Jupiter file:
Do not assume employment, a promotion, fame, or that the reader wants a larger public role.
Do not assume pregnancy, family illness, a move, or a specific home event without chart support.
Do not assume deception, addiction, spiritual awakening, or that inspiration guarantees a result.
These are generation constraints, not prose.
Do not make the reader sit through disclaimers such as:
This transit does not necessarily mean someone will become ill.
Instead, simply don't invent the illness.

18. Domain does not equal life status

Midheaven does not automatically mean employed.
Venus does not automatically mean partnered.
Moon does not automatically mean mother/family.
Mars does not automatically mean conflict.
Neptune does not automatically mean confusion.
Pluto does not automatically mean abuse.
Uranus does not automatically mean rebellion.
Jupiter does not automatically mean luck.
Generate the domain, then let the rest of the chart determine the manifestation.

19. Never backfill from information the reader reveals afterward

This remains a hard rule.
The writing must come from:
astrology → supported manifestation → prose
Never:
user tells us what happened → retrofit astrology → pretend it was predicted
Reader-reported outcomes can evaluate the forecast later. They cannot become generation evidence.

20. Your direct comments that redirected the writing

These are the editorial corrections I would preserve almost verbatim as generation guidance.
"These phrases have drifted."
Translation:
Stop optimizing for neat doctrine language. Compare the copy against the approved voice and lived prose, not against the previous generated draft.
"This is more of the style."
You said this after showing the CC screenshots.
Translation:
Add personality, rhythm, permission, and point of view. Let the copy speak to a person instead of documenting the transit.
"I need the entire write-up."
Translation:
Do not solve one aspect beautifully and leave the rest in the old register. Once the standard is established, apply it consistently across the full family.
"Please keep writing this until it's approved."
Translation:
`needs_review` means continue revising. Do not treat a technically correct draft as finished because another rewrite has already been attempted.
"If you need to do it in 4 stages do that, but complete the file."
Translation:
Large scope is not permission to lower quality or return a partial sample. Break work into controlled internal batches, then deliver a complete artifact.
Earlier recurring correction: "This is factual, but still flat."
Translation:
Accuracy is necessary but insufficient. A correct statement must also contain an observation, consequence, contradiction, cadence, or recognizable human moment.
"You just changed the position of the sentences and then flattened the sentences."
Translation:
Revision must improve the thought, not merely rearrange it. Preserve any line that already has life. Do not replace vivid prose with smoother abstraction.
"This doesn't make sense when I read it."
Translation:
Read every sentence as ordinary English, not as astrology logic. If a sentence only makes sense because the writer knows the transit mechanics, rewrite it.
"Write in a CC way that is not flat."
Translation:
Use warmth and lyrical confidence after the observation is clear. Do not confuse CC-like cadence with vagueness or therapy language.

21. The editorial test before an entry can be called finished

For every entry, ask:

1. What is actually happening?
2. Can the first sentence be recognized without knowing astrology?
3. Is there a real person, object, decision, schedule, amount, request, deadline, or consequence?
4. Does the copy show cause and consequence, not merely a domain?
5. Is there a useful contradiction?
6. Is the planet still doing its own job?
7. Is the aspect materially changing how that planetary job behaves?
8. Could this exact paragraph be pasted under five other transits? If yes, it is too generic.
9. Did I explain a good sentence after it already landed?
10. Did I end with generic wisdom?
11. Is any sentence written for the astrologer rather than the reader?
12. Did I use a keyword list where one lived scene would be stronger?
13. Did I invent a life status or event the chart did not earn?
14. Does the advice emerge from the actual circumstance?
15. Does the copy have enough personality that someone might remember one sentence from it?

If 1, 3, 4, 6, or 7 fail, it is an astrology/content failure.
If 2, 5, 8, 9, 10, 11, 12, 14, or 15 fail, it is a writing failure.
Either way, it stays `needs_review`.

22. The revision loop that got Jupiter to the approved level

Use this exact workflow for the other planetary files:
Pass 1: Astrology integrity
Confirm planet function, natal target function, and aspect distinction. Do not draft beautifully yet.
Pass 2: Remove doctrine prose
Flag every sentence that sounds like:

* "X represents…"
* "This aspect creates…"
* "Growth pushes against…"
* "Energy flows…"
* "An opportunity appears around…"
* "This transit asks you to…"

Replace it with an observable circumstance.
Pass 3: Voice pass
Add thesis, cadence, point of view, warmth, and an occasional memorable line. Do not flatten good factual material.
Pass 4: Lived-consequence pass
Replace abstract consequences with actual ones: time, money, sleep, work, home, access, credit, travel, appointments, responsibility, decisions, relationships.
Pass 5: Cut
Remove:

* explanation after observation
* keyword stacks
* generic coaching
* redundant astrology explanation
* manufactured closing wisdom

Pass 6: Full-family comparison
Read all five aspects beside each other and ask:
Could I tell which aspect this is without looking at the label?
If not, the distinctions are not strong enough.
Pass 7: Full-file consistency
Do not approve isolated good rows while the rest of the planet remains in an older register.
```
