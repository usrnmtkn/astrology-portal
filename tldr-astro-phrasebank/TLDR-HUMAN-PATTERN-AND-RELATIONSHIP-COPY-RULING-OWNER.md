# Human-pattern and relationship copy ruling (owner direction)

**Status:** `owner_directed`
**Issued:** 2026-08-21
**Scope:** Friends daily glance, Friends synastry detail, natal placement detail, and short natal-pattern cards
**Active for drafting and review:** `true`
**Reader-copy approval:** `false`
**Promotion authorized:** `false`

This is a non-reader-facing writing and review rule distilled from the owner's
2026-08-21 editorial notes. It governs how future copy is built and judged. It
does not approve any candidate wording, change any serving row, or supersede an
existing exact approval record. Candidate replacements remain `needs_review`
until the owner approves the complete exact wording and directional variants.

## Relationship language

1. Name the relationship as the `connection`. Do not use `room` as a metaphor
   for the relationship itself. A literal physical room and spatial phrases such
   as `room to breathe` remain different uses.
2. Prefer ordinary spoken phrasing over nominalized or formal phrasing. A line
   should sound natural without the reader translating it back into conversation.
3. A synastry passage remains directional: one person does something, the other
   responds, and a recognizable pattern develops. It must not turn into a generic
   explanation of an aspect.
4. The astrology explains why a pattern is easy to enter. It never excuses a
   missed call, an unmade reservation, unshared work, or another observable action.
5. Keep psychological certainty proportional to the mechanism. Do not infer a
   person's worth, history, wounds, motives, or relationship outcome from an
   aspect that only supports a communication or preference pattern.

## Do and Don't lists

1. Keep the two columns semantically separate. `Do` items name an affirmative
   action. `Don't` items name the behavior to avoid.
2. Keep grammar parallel within each column. Imperatives belong together;
   gerunds or behavior labels belong together.
3. Supply the object when a transitive action needs one. `Ask for more` is not a
   finished instruction; name more time, affection, notice, help, money, or the
   other governed object.
4. Do not guess between near-synonyms when the owner's note leaves the wording
   open. Keep the row in review until the exact verb is approved.

## Full natal placement detail

Use this movement:

1. **Placement function:** in one or two sentences, state what the placement
   governs in the person's life.
2. **Sign mechanism:** show how this sign performs that function without reducing
   the person to adjectives.
3. **Lived evidence:** show two to four observable behaviors that share the same
   mechanism.
4. **Consequence:** state what changes around the person because of those
   behaviors.
5. **Complication:** show how the same useful mechanism can become difficult,
   without diagnosing the person.
6. **Mature expression:** end with a more useful expression of that same
   mechanism, not generic self-acceptance or coaching.

After the opening, the passage must become more specific, not more abstract.
The placement explanation earns the reader's attention; the rest of the copy
shows a person they can recognize.

## Short natal-pattern cards

Short cards put the human pattern ahead of the astrology taxonomy:

1. human headline;
2. core drive;
3. healthy expression;
4. two to four plausible manifestations of the same mechanism;
5. complication created by overextending that mechanism;
6. observable consequence;
7. developmental direction grounded in the same mechanism.

The astrology may appear as a compact label or opening attribution, then moves
backstage. Do not re-explain it after the pattern has been established. Multiple
manifestations create breadth, but they remain possibilities and may not invent
a personal history. Borrow the architecture of psychologically recognizable
pattern writing, not its strongest unsupported claims.

## Review questions

- Does the relationship copy still identify who acts and who responds?
- Does the astrology explain the pattern without excusing the behavior?
- Does every `Do` instruction name enough of an action to be usable?
- Are `Do` and `Don't` items grammatically parallel within their own columns?
- Does a natal passage become more behavioral after its opening?
- Does the complication grow from the same mechanism as the strength?
- Could the copy make the same psychological claim under several unrelated
  aspects or placements? If so, it is too generic or too certain.
