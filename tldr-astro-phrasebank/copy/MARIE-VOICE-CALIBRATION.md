## B. Voice signature distilled from the above

1. **House-activation opener, plainly:** "This Full Moon activates your 5th house of creative expression, showing where you've been…". Names the placement + the house's theme in one clause, then goes straight to a behavior. (My clauses already do this — confirmed.)
2. **Dry, slightly irreverent asides:** "That's nice of you, but will drag after a while." / "kinda unhinged, anyway." / "It's just burnout." She undercuts earnestness with a wry beat. **My clauses are cleaner and more earnest than this.** ← main delta.
3. **Hyper-specific relational micro-scenes:** not "a relationship," but "the coworker who got cold when you stopped covering for them," "the friends who needed you through their divorce but couldn't be bothered with yours." **My lived-scenes are concrete but more general.** ← delta.
4. **Blunt "Stop X" imperatives** and reframes as closers: "Stop proving your worth to people who can't even recognize their own."
5. **Names grief and the costly pattern to release:** "Grieve the version of you who worked so hard to make broken things work."
6. **Somatic:** "Some truths are felt in the body before the mind can understand them."
7. **Casual register:** contractions throughout, "way more," "kinda," "a while," em-dash-free, present tense, second person. Occasional one-word or two-word imperative punch ("Be feral.").

## C. Verdict on the 391 clauses

Structurally and tonally **on-voice** — the house/aspect-locate opener, one lived behavior, one proportionate action, and the release-the-costly-pattern move all match. The gap is **register, not structure**: mine run ~10% more earnest and more general than Marie's actual copy, which is dryer, funnier, and hyper-specific. This is a **light tone pass, not a rewrite.** Concretely:

- Add one wry undercut where the surface allows (transit/daily cards, not eclipse/grief copy).
- Push lived-scene specificity toward named micro-scenarios (the coworker, the group chat, the unanswered text) rather than "a relationship / a conversation."
- Favor blunt "Stop / Grieve / Say" imperatives in the action slot.
- Keep the wry beat OUT of heavy surfaces (eclipse, loss, Pluto/Saturn hard aspects) — there she stays plain and compassionate (see A2).

## F. Per-surface voice (from Marie's articles — 19 full pieces on hand)

Voice is **surface-specific**. Two rules confirmed against her published articles (Full Moon in Aries, Venus in Virgo, etc.):

1. **Sky / collective = first-person plural ("we / us / our").** Not detached third person. Her articles: "Mars is asking *us* to fight for emotional safety"; "*we're* asked to release *our* fantasies"; "*We've* been practicing the art of relationship." → All 120 `sky.planet_sign` `collective_shift` lines are authored in "we." (Was: "The mood turns future-facing…" → now: "We turn future-facing…")
2. **Personalized horoscope = "You've been … This week/month …" with a concrete scene.** Her per-sign horoscopes open with a lived pattern and move to a specific, datable moment; never a vague verb like "tugs at you." "You've been practicing speaking up… Maybe you've gotten better at setting boundaries at work but still struggle with family dynamics." → All 120 `home.planetary_horoscope` `home_scene` lines follow this. (Was: "…tugs at you" → now: "You've been circling a bigger question… This week it gets concrete: a class, a trip…")

Banned in these surfaces: vague pull-verbs ("tugs at you," "calls to you," "beckons"), and third-person detachment on Sky copy.

## E. Clarity rules (from Marie's sign-off feedback)

Dryness must never cost clarity. Two rules added after review:

1. **No undecodable metaphor.** "Stop grading the whole thing by its coldest hour" was rejected — the reader shouldn't have to decode an image. Say it plainly: "let people show you they care through what they actually do." Prefer **"directly"** over "out loud."
2. **Every pronoun needs a clear referent.** "It'll survive" was rejected — what is "it"? Name it: "Everyone will be fine." No floating "it / the thing / this" the reader has to resolve.

(These extend the voice spec's existing ban on vague placeholders. The seam filter can't catch them — they read as fluent — so they belong on the human sign-off checklist.)
