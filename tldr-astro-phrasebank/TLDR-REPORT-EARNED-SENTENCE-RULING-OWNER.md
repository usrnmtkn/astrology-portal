## The rule

```text
Clarity is the floor, not the goal.

Every sentence must be understandable on the first read. That requirement
never relaxes. But a report made only of clear, correct, careful sentences
is not finished. It is safe.

The owner's writing is clear AND alive at the same time. The strongest
sentences do both jobs in one move: they say something ordinary in ordinary
words, and they land.

"You can know that a chapter is over and still miss the person you were
inside it."

Nothing in that sentence needs decoding. It is also the sentence a reader
remembers. That is the target: not clear INSTEAD OF alive. Clear AND alive.
```

## What makes a sentence earn its keep

A sentence earns its place when it does at least one of these, in plain language:

1. **Holds two true things at once.** "You can still be fully capable of doing a job and realize you cannot keep organizing your week around it." "You may genuinely want both and still find the same hours cannot belong to both."
2. **Names the consequence nobody says out loud.** "Their expectation does not put the missing hours back into the week." "One easy yes can become three hours you did not have."
3. **Says the ordinary thing exactly.** "The appointment is still at ten. Work still starts in the morning." "A career can change before the résumé does."
4. **Gives permission or judgment the reader was waiting for.** "Overcommitting doesn't always happen because of bad choices. It usually happens when you say yes to good things." "Some families only love the version of a person that never holds them accountable."

## The unit requirement

Every substantive prose unit contains at least one sentence that meets the standard above. A unit made entirely of accurate, clear, careful sentences with nothing a reader would remember is **incomplete**, not acceptable. The absence is a named defect (`no_earned_sentence`), not a stylistic preference.

This does not license decoration. A sentence that reaches for effect and fails the clarity floor fails outright; the no-cleverness-tax ruling governs that case unchanged. The two rules are a gate with two sides: nothing gets through that is unclear, and nothing gets through that is merely inoffensive.
