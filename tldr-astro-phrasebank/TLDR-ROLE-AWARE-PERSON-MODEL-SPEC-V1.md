# TLDR role-aware person model v1

Status: implementation contract for shared fragments; bulk row migration is not authorized.

## Purpose

Shared fragments must identify the person role behind each reference. The renderer derives grammar from the role, the surface, the person's stated pronouns, and the passage-level reference policy. It never replaces pronoun strings globally.

The minimum roles are:

- `chartSubject`: the person whose chart the fragment describes;
- `viewer`: the person reading the surface;
- `otherPerson`: another person participating in the described relationship.

On Self, `chartSubject` and `viewer` are the same person and render as `you`, `your`, `yours`, and `yourself`. On Friend, they are different: `viewer` remains second person, while `chartSubject` uses the friend's display name and stated pronouns. `otherPerson` is independently supplied and inflected.

## Tagged fragment grammar

Person-bearing text uses role tokens such as:

- `{{chartSubject.subject}}`, `{{chartSubject.object}}`, `{{chartSubject.possessiveAdjective}}`, and `{{chartSubject.reflexive}}`;
- corresponding `viewer.*` and `otherPerson.*` forms;
- agreement tokens `{{chartSubject.bePresent}}`, `{{chartSubject.havePresent}}`, or `{{chartSubject.verb:tend}}`.

An ordinary present-tense verb must be emitted through an agreement token. The renderer distinguishes `Nikki tends`, `they tend`, `she tends`, and `you tend`. A verb or agreement token before its role reference fails closed.

Untagged personal pronouns and legacy person slots are ambiguous. They fail closed and enter author review; the compiler may not guess which role they represent.

## Name and pronoun policy

The policy is `name-first-then-pronoun` per passage:

1. The first Friend reference to `chartSubject` or `otherPerson` uses the display name (or possessive name).
2. Later references use the person's stated pronouns.
3. `viewer` always remains `you` on both surfaces.
4. If a pronoun preference is absent, the app's existing default applies. A supplied `she`, `he`, or `they` preference is never overwritten.

The renderer state is shared across the fragments composing one passage so every template does not restart with the name.

## Scope boundary

Role-aware inflection makes shared **fragments** safe to compose on Self and Friend surfaces. It does not authorize a Friend **passage** to be generated by inflecting or structurally copying a Self passage.

`TLDR-VOICE-ENTRY-POINT-RULING-OWNER.md` remains controlling: Self enters through the reader's experience; Friend is separately authored from what observers can see. The blocking `pronoun-swap-derivation` gate remains in force for passage-level content.

## Fail-closed rules

Rendering refuses when:

- a personal pronoun is not role-tagged;
- a used role is undeclared or a declared role is unused;
- `otherPerson` is used but not supplied;
- a role is introduced with a reflexive or possessive pronoun instead of a name/reference;
- an agreement token appears before the reference whose agreement it needs;
- a role-bearing token is unknown;
- the content unit is a passage rather than a `shared_fragment`.
