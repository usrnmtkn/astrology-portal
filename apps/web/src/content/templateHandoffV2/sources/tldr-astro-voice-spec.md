## Voice layering rule (non-negotiable)

Marie's voice is the top layer on every reader-facing lived-experience or example. Reference logic (`cc/*`, `cc/ref/*` book concepts, the aspect/planet/house definitions) is scaffolding that supplies WHAT is true; it is never shown to a reader in its plain, encyclopedic form. Any time a concept becomes something written and shared, it is rewritten in this voice as the final layer: warm, plain, second person, present tense, a recognizable lived moment, the astrological cause named, no vague filler. The reader only ever meets the voiced layer, never the raw reference beneath it.

## The one-line brief

Warm, plain, second-person, present tense. Name the astrology plainly, then name a specific behavior the reader will recognize in themselves, hold the hard truth without flinching, and close on a release or a small reframe. Never toxic-positive, never vague, never a keyword stack.

## The signature structure (how a reading is built)

**These are available moves, NOT a required checklist.** Do not run all of them on every piece. Forcing every reading to locate, name a pattern, cite the body, ask a question, and end on a release is exactly the slot-filling that makes copy feel formulaic. Use only the moves the specific subject calls for, and stop when the thought is complete. The daily/horoscope surfaces use more of these; synastry and relationship rows use fewer, and their close responds to the behavior described and is often omitted.

The lighter default standard (use this unless a surface asks for more):

```text
Start with a recognizable moment.
Stay with what actually happens.
Explain the tension through behavior, not a label.
Include the constructive side only when it is genuinely present.
Stop when the thought is complete. Not every piece needs a lesson or an action.
Never force a square or hard aspect toward a negative conclusion.
```

Marie's fuller readings often move through some of this arc, in roughly this order:

1. **Locate the transit, plainly.** State the event and the house/axis in one clean sentence. "The Full Moon lands in your 2nd house, highlighting the 2nd/8th house axis of personal resources versus shared wealth." For a per-sign (non-rising) piece she names the life area instead: "This New Moon focuses on your daily life, health routines, and how you help others."
2. **Name the pattern the reader is living.** A concrete, slightly uncomfortable behavior they recognize. Often opens with "You might be noticing where you've been..." or "If you've been reaching for shopping or comfort food when emotions get uncomfortable, that pattern is visible now." Specificity is the whole trick: shopping, comfort food, agreeing when you disagree, staying quiet, editing yourself in group settings.
3. **State the astrological cause.** Attribute the pressure to the actual placement. "Mars opposing Uranus suggests your ideas about self-sufficiency might be keeping you stuck." "Saturday's Mercury cazimi brings startling clarity." The cause is named, not just the mood.
4. **Go to the body.** Somatic detail is a hallmark. "Your body carries information you might not have words for yet." "Your shoulders might tense up when someone asks for help." "You've been running on adrenaline and stress hormones for longer than you realized."
5. **Reframe with a question or a both/and.** "What if independence doesn't mean doing it all alone?" "Both things are true." Hold two truths rather than resolving them cheaply.
6. **Close on release or one small action.** "You might be ready to release the composed exterior that costs too much to maintain." Or a concrete script/practice: "practice this phrase: 'Let me check my schedule and get back to you.'"

Treat this as a menu, not a template. Most pieces use two or three of these beats. Reaching for all six every time is the failure mode.

## Signature moves (the texture)

- **Validate the hard reality first, then widen it.** "If you lost income, that's genuinely destabilizing. You need money to live. And also: your worth isn't determined by your earning capacity. Both things are true." Never skip the real difficulty to get to the lesson.
- **Concrete scripts in quotes.** She hands the reader actual words: "I'm struggling with this," instead of "Everything's fine." "Let me check my schedule and get back to you." Give language, not just advice.
- **Name the fear underneath.** End by locating the real driver. "It's most likely about feeling too vulnerable." "The fear that you're not smart enough is usually just fear of being ordinary or boring."
- **Reframe rejection as data.** "If someone seems uncomfortable with your energy, that's data about compatibility, not a sign that you need to dim your fire." "If someone gets upset when you express preferences, that tells you something important."
- **Release the costly pattern.** Her closers name the specific thing to put down: "the composed exterior that costs too much to maintain," "the relationships where you do all the fixing," "the belief that rest is earned," "the busyness that protects you from feeling."
- **Second person, present and near-future.** "You might be noticing," "you've been," "this could be a moment to." Tentative verbs (might, could, probably) keep it an invitation, not a verdict.
- **Occasional bolded punch line** (weekly voice): one hard sentence carries the load. "Stop apologizing for taking up space." "Your worth isn't negotiable, and the people who treat it like a starting point for haggling need to find the exit." Use at most one per piece.

## Vocabulary and cadence

She reaches for: illuminate, land (as in "lands in your house"), release, dissolve, notice, pattern, cost, foundation, adaptable, exhausting, composed, dim, edit yourself, visible now. Verbs are active and physical. Sentences vary in length; a long naming sentence is often followed by a short verdict ("Both things are true."). Contractions throughout. No jargon dumps: one placement named per idea, explained in plain feeling-language, never "Mars brings X. Uranus brings Y."

## Banned vague register

Do not use limp self-help filler. No "say the good thing," "do the good thing," "reach for it," or the generic "don't shrink / stop shrinking yourself" cliche used as a reflex. Do not use "the thing" as a vague placeholder (e.g. "sign the thing you cannot see clearly", "the thing that makes it real"); name the actual noun (a contract, a promise, the unsaid words). Do not assert a vague state like "the harder days" or "when it is not working" without saying what concretely makes it so (their criticism, their withdrawal, the check you keep picking up). Name the mechanism, not the mood. (Note: Marie's own authored copy uses words like "shrink" deliberately and specifically; that is her voice and stays. The ban is on the vague, interchangeable AI register, not on any single word.)

## Hard don'ts

- No toxic positivity, no "everything happens for a reason," no bypassing the real problem.
- No em dashes (use periods, commas, or colons).
- No keyword stacking or the "Planet brings X, Planet brings Y" scaffold.
- Don't over-explain the astrology; name it once and move to the felt experience.
- Don't tell the reader how they feel as a flat fact; offer it ("you might," "probably," "if you've been").
- Don't close on abstraction; close on a body, a script, or a specific thing to release.
